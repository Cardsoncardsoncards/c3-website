Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty landed on 18 February 2022, folding the plane's classic spirit world aesthetic into a cyberpunk future and giving Magic players a set stuffed with channel lands, mythic planeswalkers and a legendary cast of samurai, ninja and rogue AI. Almost three years on, the set's price ladder has settled into a shape that rewards looking closely rather than just glancing at set boosters, and this list is our attempt to lay that shape out plainly.

This ranking covers the twenty most expensive cards in the set by Australian dollar price, ordered from number one down to number twenty. Where a card has several printings within Neon Dynasty, whether that is a regular non-foil, a foil, a borderless treatment or a promotional version, we rank it by whichever printing carries the highest price, then list the other printings underneath. If two printings happen to land on exactly the same figure, that tie is noted rather than quietly broken by us picking one arbitrarily.

A handful of cards on this list carry four or even five printings, and in most of those cases only three or four of them actually carry a tracked price at the time of writing, which is itself worth noting rather than glossing over. Every figure here is a snapshot. Magic singles prices in Australia drift with supply, with reprints, and with shifts in what the format demands, so the numbers below are accurate to when this page was written rather than a permanent state of affairs.

Boseiju, Who Endures sits at the top of the list, and by a comfortable margin over the card in second place. From there the list runs down through channel lands, mythic legends and a scattering of rare utility cards, closing out with a common that only exists as one priced printing. Readers looking to buy any of these can do so through the card pages on our own site or through eBay Australia.

**How does this list handle cards with multiple printings?**
Each card appears once and is ranked by whichever of its printings carries the highest price. Any other printings of that card are then listed alongside it rather than treated as separate entries.

**What happens if two printings share the exact same price?**
Where that occurs the tie is noted plainly rather than being broken silently in either direction, so readers can see the two printings sit level.
