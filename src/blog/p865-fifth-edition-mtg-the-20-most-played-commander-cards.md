Fifth Edition landed on 24 March 1997, one of the old core sets that reprinted staples from earlier Magic: The Gathering releases rather than introducing a wave of new cards. It sits in that stretch of the game's history where core sets acted as a reprint vehicle, pulling powerful and efficient cards forward so new players could find them at retail rather than hunting through older expansions. That reprint role is exactly why so many Commander staples turn up in its checklist decades later.

This list ranks the twenty most played Fifth Edition cards in Commander today, using EDHREC rank as the measure. EDHREC tracks how often a card shows up across published Commander decklists, and a lower number means the card appears in more of them, so rank 16 is more played than rank 500. The list is ordered from number 1, the most played card, down to number 20, the least played of this particular twenty.

Where a card had more than one printing within Fifth Edition, the methodology takes the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across those printings, and prices the card by whichever printing carries the highest price. If two printings happen to land on exactly the same price, that tie is noted rather than resolved by some arbitrary tiebreaker. As it turns out, every card on this particular list has a single priced printing in the set, so there are no ties or printing gaps to work through here, but the rule stands for any set where that changes.

One more thing worth saying plainly: prices move. What is quoted here is a snapshot taken at one point in time, and singles markets for older cards can shift with reprints elsewhere, tournament relevance, or simple supply drying up. Treat the figures as a reference point, not a promise of what you will pay next month.
