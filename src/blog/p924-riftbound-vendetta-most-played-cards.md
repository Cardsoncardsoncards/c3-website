Riftbound Vendetta released on 31 July 2026, which gives us roughly four weeks of real tournament data instead of preview season theorycrafting. We wrote about [the first week](/blog/riftbound-vendetta-meta-first-week/) and said the early read was that Vendetta had not displaced Unleashed. A month on, that assessment has largely held, and the detail underneath it is more interesting than the headline.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The dataset referenced below covers 16 tournaments with 64 or more competitors held since Vendetta's release. That is a reasonable sample for a month-old set but it is not a large one, and event-level results for a game this young are noisier than the equivalent numbers in an established game. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

Kennen, Heart of the Tempest leads the metagame at roughly 9 per cent, followed by Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman and Irelia, Blade Dancer. Across 16 tournaments with 64 or more players since release, 25 different legends have recorded at least one Top 8, and only four of those are from Vendetta: Kennen, Nasus, Mel and Ambessa. Kennen and Nasus have performed well, Mel sits behind Ezreal and Diana, and Ambessa has not established itself as a serious competitive option. The cards doing the actual work in these decks are commons and uncommons costing under AU$1.

## The dataset and its limits

Stating this properly, because usage rankings are easy to publish and hard to publish honestly.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Set release | 31 July 2026 |
| Window covered | Release to 26 August 2026, roughly four weeks |
| Tournaments in sample | 16, each with 64 or more competitors |
| Distinct legends with a Top 8 | 25 |
| Of those, from Vendetta | 4 |
| Metric | Metashare and Top 8 appearances |

Two caveats that matter. First, metashare measures what people brought, not what won. A 9 per cent metashare tells you Kennen was the most popular choice, not that it was the best one. Second, we do not have per-card inclusion counts across the full sample, so the card-level section below reflects the cards that established deck primers and event coverage consistently identify, rather than a counted frequency table. We would rather tell you that than present a ranked list with invented percentages.

## The legends people are actually playing

**Kennen, Heart of the Tempest** leads at roughly 9 per cent metashare. It is the standout Vendetta legend and the clearest case of the new set genuinely adding a top-tier option.

**Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman** and **Irelia, Blade Dancer** follow. Both predate Vendetta.

That ordering is the finding. One month after a new set, the top three legends are one new arrival and two holdovers, and 21 of the 25 legends posting Top 8 results come from earlier sets.

- **[Nasus, Ascended](/cards/riftbound/vendetta-nasus-ascended-046-166)**, AU$1.00. Has looked strong in results, the second genuine Vendetta success.

[Ezreal, Prodigy](/cards/riftbound/spiritforged-ezreal-prodigy-149-221) at AU$10.40 and [Diana, No Longer Human](/cards/riftbound/unleashed-diana-no-longer-human-149-219) at AU$1.65 are the incumbents it has failed to displace.

Two of four new legends working is a reasonable outcome for a set. It is not the format reset that release-week coverage anticipated.

## Old cards versus new cards

The most instructive detail in a month of Vendetta data concerns Irelia, and it is worth dwelling on.

Irelia, Blade Dancer remains a top three legend, and community testing has settled on SFD-048 as the most important four-cost unit in the deck. The staple Irelia list barely changed compared with the previous set. A new set landed, and one of the format's best decks essentially ignored it.

For Kennen the standard build centres on OGN-195-083, a card from Origins rather than from Vendetta.

That is the pattern: Vendetta added two viable legends and a handful of genuinely good cheap spells, and it slotted them into an existing framework. It did not replace the framework. The base [Irelia, Blade Dancer](/cards/riftbound/spiritforged-irelia-blade-dancer-195-221) is AU$0.35 and [Master Yi, Wuju Master](/cards/riftbound/unleashed-master-yi-wuju-master-191-219) is AU$0.19, so the incumbents are not being held in place by cost either.

## Surprises

**Ambessa underperforming.** A named, heavily promoted legend that has not converted. Promotion is not performance.

**Kennen at only 9 per cent.** For a set-leading legend that is a modest share. Compare the [MTG Standard picture after the August bans](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/), where the top deck sits at 13 per cent, or the pre-ban situation there where three decks held over half the metagame. Riftbound's format is genuinely diverse, with 25 legends posting Top 8 results.

**How little the top lists changed.** The expectation after a release is disruption. The observation is continuity.

## What is likely to persist

Cards that survive a format's first month tend to survive longer, and the ones above have the best claim:

- **[Up from the Deep](/cards/riftbound/vendetta-up-from-the-deep-100-166)** and **[Twilight Shroud](/cards/riftbound/vendetta-twilight-shroud-031-166)** are described as glue and enabler cards rather than as win conditions. Cards that make strategies work outlast cards that are strategies.
- **[Applied Researchers](/cards/riftbound/vendetta-applied-researchers-055-166)** affects resource maths, which is the most durable kind of effect in any card game.
- **Kennen and Nasus** have four weeks of results behind them across a 16 tournament sample.

Least likely to persist: Ambessa, on current evidence, and Mel unless something displaces Ezreal or Diana.

## What a month of data can and cannot tell you

Riftbound is a young game, and it is worth being explicit about what that does to the reliability of everything above.

**Sixteen tournaments is a workable sample, not a large one.** In an established game, a month of results at this scale would be one input among many, cross-checked against online ladder data and a deep history of prior formats. Riftbound does not have that history yet. There is no long-run baseline to say whether a 9 per cent metashare leader is normal for this game or unusually low.

**Player skill and deck strength are still tangled.** In a game roughly a year into organised play, the strongest players have disproportionate influence over which decks post results. A legend can look strong because good players chose it rather than because it is strong. Separating those requires more data than exists.

**The field is still learning.** Four weeks is not enough time for the community to solve a set. Ambessa underperforming today may mean it is weak, or it may mean nobody has found the right build. The Irelia example cuts the same way in reverse: that deck is stable because a year of testing has settled it, not because it is inherently better than the alternatives.

None of that makes the data useless. It makes it provisional, which is a different thing. The safest reading is that Kennen and Nasus have shown enough to be worth playing, that Ambessa has not shown enough yet, and that the case is open rather than closed on all three.
