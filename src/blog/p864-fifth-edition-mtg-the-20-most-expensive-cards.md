Fifth Edition is one of the core sets that shaped how Magic: The Gathering was sold and played through the mid to late 1990s, released on the 24th of March 1997. It gathered together reprints of cards from across the game's early history, which is why the checklist ranges from cheap commons up to some genuinely significant rares that still carry a price today. This post looks specifically at the 20 most expensive cards in the set, ranked by their current price in Australian dollars.

Where a card has more than one printing within Fifth Edition, the ranking uses whichever printing carries the highest price, and if two printings happen to land on exactly the same figure, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved one way or another. Most of the cards on this list, as it turns out, have only a single priced printing in the set, so the methodology mostly matters as a rule for the exceptions. Game of Chaos is the one card here with more than one printing recorded, though only one of those printings carries a price.

Mana Vault sits at the top of the list, and by a wide margin, at AU$139.42. Everything below it falls away fairly quickly, with the gap between second and twentieth place covering a much narrower band of prices. As with any singles market, these figures are a snapshot taken at a point in time. Card prices move, sometimes gradually and sometimes quickly, so treat the numbers below as a read of the market now rather than a permanent ranking.
