The Hobbit arrived in Standard under unusually messy conditions. Wizards banned three cards on 10 August 2026, four days before the set released on 14 August, which means the format that greeted The Hobbit was not the format anyone had been testing against. Untangling what the new set did from what the bans did is the whole analytical problem here, and most coverage has not bothered to try.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The dataset underpinning this article is Wizards' own Metagame Mentor analysis of nearly 300 successful tournament decks from 11 to 17 August 2026, drawn from all published Magic Online Standard Challenge decklists in that window plus several dozen top RCQ decklists from around the world. That is a one week sample from the first week of a reset format. It is the best evidence available and it is still early evidence.

## Quick answer

Smaug the Magnificent is the most-played new card from The Hobbit in post-ban Standard, appearing in seven Mono-Red Aggro decks and six Boros Dragons decks in the sample. But no Hobbit card is carrying a top-tier archetype. The top three decks in the first post-ban week, Mono-Green Landfall at 13.0 per cent, Dimir Midrange at 10.6 per cent and Mardu Discard at 9.8 per cent, are not Hobbit decks. The set's real effect has been to strengthen fringe strategies, particularly Boros Dwarves and Five-Color Humans, rather than to define the format. The format opened up because three cards were banned, not because a Universes Beyond set arrived.

## What was banned, and why it matters here

On 10 August 2026, Wizards banned three cards in Standard:

- [Badgermole Cub](/cards/mtg/badgermole-cub)
- [Stormchaser's Talent](/cards/mtg/stormchasers-talent)
- [Gran-Gran](/cards/mtg/gran-gran)

Wizards' stated reasoning centred on Selesnya Offense, which was posting over a 57 per cent win rate while holding the second-highest play rate in the format. Their published position was that decks combining high win rates with high play rates are the largest threats to format balance. Badgermole Cub was described as demanding immediate answers and creating an insurmountable mana advantage, Stormchaser's Talent as inappropriately threatening for a one mana card, and Gran-Gran as providing excessive late game value for its cost.

The three most prominent decks immediately before the bans were Izzet Prowess, Selesnya Offense and Jeskai Lessons, which together made up over half the winner's metagame. All three lost a key card on 10 August.

**This is the confounder.** Any claim that The Hobbit changed Standard has to contend with the fact that half the winning metagame was dismantled four days earlier. We have covered the ban and its market effects separately in [the MTG August 2026 ban list](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/).

| 2 | Dimir Midrange | 10.6% |
| 3 | Mardu Discard | 9.8% |
| 4 | Izzet Spellementals | 8.9% |
| 5 | Orzhov Life Gain | 8.3% |
| 6 | Jeskai Lessons | 6.9% |
| 7 | Dimir Excruciator | 5.9% |
| 8 | Four-Color Control | 5.8% |
| 9 | Mono-Red Aggro | 5.0% |
| 10 | Azorius Prison | 4.3% |
| 11 | Boros Dragons | 3.7% |
| 12 | Sultai Reanimator | 2.0% |
| 13 to 16 | Four further archetypes | 1.9% to 0.9% |
| 17 | Other | 9.8% |

Read the shape rather than the ranking. Sixteen named archetypes, none above 13 per cent, and a long tail. Compare that to the pre-ban picture where three decks held over half the winner's metagame between them. That is a format that opened up substantially, and the opening is attributable to the bans.

Note also that Jeskai Lessons is still present at 6.9 per cent despite losing Gran-Gran, and Mono-Green Landfall leads the format despite losing Badgermole Cub. Wizards' own analysis observes that Badgermole Cub was never an essential piece of the Landfall deck. Bans reshuffle formats. They do not always kill the decks they touch.

## Where The Hobbit actually shows up

### Smaug the Magnificent

[Smaug the Magnificent](/cards/mtg/smaug-the-magnificent) is the most-played new card from the set. In the sample it appears in seven Mono-Red Aggro decks and six Boros Dragons decks, alongside [Magmatic Hellkite](/cards/mtg/magmatic-hellkite) and [Nova Hellkite](/cards/mtg/nova-hellkite) in the Dragon shells.

That is genuine, verifiable Standard play in two archetypes. It is also worth keeping in proportion: Boros Dragons sits at 3.7 per cent and Mono-Red Aggro at 5.0 per cent. Smaug is a real card in real decks that are not, at present, at the top of the format.

Smaug now costs AU$73.82, down from AU$310.22 at preorder. Being the most-played new card in the format did not stop a 76 per cent fall, which is a useful corrective to the idea that competitive play props up a card's price. We laid out the full picture in [The Hobbit preorder versus post-release prices](/blog/the-hobbit-preorder-vs-post-release-prices/).

### Boros Dwarves is the set's real contribution

The archetype most enabled by The Hobbit is Dwarves, and it is built almost entirely from cheap cards:

- [Thorin, Mountain-king](/cards/mtg/thorin-mountain-king), AU$39.97
- [Kíli the Resourceful](/cards/mtg/k-li-the-resourceful), AU$3.71
- [Dáin's Company](/cards/mtg/d-ins-company), AU$2.30
- [Dáin Ironfoot](/cards/mtg/d-in-ironfoot), AU$1.19
- [Bofur, Reliable Guardian // Concerted Care](/cards/mtg/bofur-reliable-guardian-concerted-care), AU$0.48
- [Thorin Oakenshield](/cards/mtg/thorin-oakenshield), AU$0.43
- [Dwarven Mauler](/cards/mtg/dwarven-mauler), AU$0.28
- [Dwarven Mattock](/cards/mtg/dwarven-mattock), AU$0.24
- [The Lonely Mountain](/cards/mtg/the-lonely-mountain), AU$26.96

Everything below Thorin and The Lonely Mountain is effectively free. A Dwarves shell is one of the cheapest new archetypes to assemble in Standard right now, and it is the clearest case of The Hobbit adding something the format did not previously have.

### Five-Color Humans

[The Queen of Dale](/cards/mtg/the-queen-of-dale) at AU$44.63 and [Key to the Side-Door](/cards/mtg/key-to-the-side-door) at AU$0.34 both appear in Five-Color Humans lists. The Queen of Dale is notable as the second-best price performer in the entire set, down only 41.1 per cent from preorder, and having a genuine competitive home is very likely part of why.

### Azorius Oculus

[Most Decrepit Old Bird // Speak Secrets](/cards/mtg/most-decrepit-old-bird-speak-secrets) at AU$0.77 shows up in Azorius Oculus.

## Separating the set from the ban

Here is the honest accounting, stated as a causal claim with its evidence.

**What the bans did.** They removed three cards from the three decks that held over half the winner's metagame, and the format immediately spread across sixteen archetypes with no deck above 13 per cent. This is a large, clearly attributable effect.

**What The Hobbit did.** It contributed the most-played new card and enabled two to three fringe archetypes, none of which is currently in the top eight by share. This is a real but modest effect.

**What we cannot separate.** Whether Boros Dwarves and Five-Color Humans would have found space without the bans. Almost certainly they had more room to appear in an opened format than they would have had against pre-ban Izzet Prowess and Selesnya Offense. The set and the ban are working together, and one week of data cannot cleanly apportion credit between them.

Anyone telling you The Hobbit reshaped Standard is overstating it. Anyone telling you the set had no impact is also wrong. The accurate version is less quotable: the bans made room and The Hobbit partly filled it.

## What the banned cards were actually holding up

It is worth looking at the three banned cards themselves, because their prices say something about how the format has absorbed the change.

[Badgermole Cub](/cards/mtg/badgermole-cub) still sits at AU$89.52, which is the highest price of any card discussed in this article, Hobbit cards included. That is not a Standard price any more. It is Commander, Pioneer and cross-format demand holding the card up after a Standard ban, and it is a good reminder that a Standard ban is not a value event for a card with homes elsewhere.

[Stormchaser's Talent](/cards/mtg/stormchasers-talent) at AU$5.32 and [Gran-Gran](/cards/mtg/gran-gran) at AU$0.41 tell the opposite story. Both were far more Standard-dependent, and both now sit at prices that reflect a much narrower set of homes.

The practical lesson for anyone holding cards through a ban announcement is that the format breadth of a card matters more than the ban itself. We go into the market side of this properly in [the August 2026 ban list winners and losers](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/).

## The card demand picture

The cards from this set with the strongest claim to holding value are the ones with competitive homes: Smaug, The Queen of Dale and Thorin, Mountain-king. All three appear in the sample. All three are also down heavily from preorder, which tells you how far preorder pricing had run ahead of reality rather than telling you these cards are weak.

If you are buying to play rather than to speculate, this is a good moment. The Dwarves package costs a few dollars beyond its two headline cards, and Standard's most expensive Hobbit card is now under AU$75.

## What the next events will test

- **Whether Mono-Green Landfall holds.** Leading a format one week after losing a card to a ban is a strong signal, but a one week sample is a one week sample.
- **Whether Boros Dwarves converts.** Appearing in successful lists and winning events are different achievements. Dwarves needs a top finish at a substantial event to move from interesting to real.
- **Whether Smaug climbs.** Seven and six deck appearances in a 300 deck sample is a foothold, not a position. If Dragons rises above its current 3.7 per cent, Smaug's price has support.
- **The RCQ season proper.** The bans landed four days before Standard RCQs opened, so the first several weeks of RCQ results will be the first genuinely large physical dataset for this format.
