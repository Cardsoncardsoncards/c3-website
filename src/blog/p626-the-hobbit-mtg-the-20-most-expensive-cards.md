The Hobbit is a Magic: The Gathering set drawing on Tolkien's earlier tale, arriving after the earlier Lord of the Rings crossovers and set for release on 2026-08-14. Because it hasn't reached shelves yet, every figure in this list is a preorder price, and preorder prices are always in motion as print runs, sealed product allocations and early demand estimates shift ahead of the actual release.

This list ranks the twenty most expensive cards in the set by their Australian dollar price. Where a card has multiple printings within The Hobbit, such as a borderless alternate or a serialised version, we rank it by whichever printing carries the highest price, and we note the other printings and their prices separately rather than folding them into a single average. If two printings of the same card land on exactly the same price, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved.

Rarity varies across the list, from mythic rares that dominate the top end down to a handful of rares that still command prices in the tens of dollars. Card by card, the blurbs below focus on how each entry sits against its neighbours on the list and what the spread between its printings implies, since the pricing figures themselves are already printed directly above each entry.

Remember throughout that these are preorder numbers tied to a set that has not yet released. Prices commonly shift, sometimes substantially, once a set is in players' hands and supply becomes clearer, so treat this list as a snapshot rather than a fixed ranking.

## Frequently asked questions

**When does The Hobbit set release?**
The Hobbit is scheduled for release on 2026-08-14. Because that date hasn't arrived yet, every price in this list is a preorder price rather than a post-release market price.

**Which is the most expensive card in The Hobbit?**
Smaug the Magnificent tops the list at AU$307.50, well ahead of the second-place card. It has four printings in the set, though only two of them carry a tracked price.

**How does the ranking handle cards with multiple printings?**
Each card appears once on the list regardless of how many printings it has in the set. It is ranked by whichever of its printings carries the highest price, with the other printings and their prices noted separately rather than averaged in.

**What happens when two printings, or two cards, share the same price?**
Ties are noted rather than silently broken. Bilbo's Gambit, Great Gilded Boat and Riddles in the Dark, for example, all share an identical headline price of AU$53.95, and that tie is called out explicitly rather than resolved by an arbitrary tiebreaker.

**Are these prices final?**

**Why do some cards have printings with no listed price?**
