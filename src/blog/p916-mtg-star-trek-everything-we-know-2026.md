Magic: The Gathering meets Star Trek on 13 November 2026, timed to the franchise's 60th anniversary. It is a full premier-style Universes Beyond release rather than a Secret Lair or a Commander-only product, and it carries one genuinely unusual collector element: seven Star Trek actors have hand-signed headliner cards, at roughly 250 copies each, inserted randomly into Collector Boosters. This guide sets out what is officially confirmed and marks clearly what is not.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The set is eleven weeks from release. Prices below are preorder asking prices on an unreleased set, and are labelled as such. If you want the ranked price lists instead of the product and mechanics overview, we maintain those separately: [Star Trek MTG most expensive cards](/blog/star-trek-mtg-most-expensive-cards/) and [Star Trek MTG most played Commander cards](/blog/star-trek-mtg-most-played-commander-cards/). This article covers the set itself.

## Quick answer

Star Trek releases 13 November 2026, with prerelease events 6 to 12 November and MTG Arena availability from 10 November. It is a Universes Beyond premier-style set made with CBS, draftable, with Play Boosters, Collector Boosters and four preconstructed Commander decks, plus a Beam Me Up Bundle, Scene Boxes and a Beginner Box. Two new keywords debut, Assimilate and Face a Dilemma, and Spacecraft and station return from Edge of Eternities. The headline collector element is seven hand-signed cards at approximately 250 copies each. On current preorder pricing the most expensive card is [Crystalline Entity](/cards/mtg/crystalline-entity) at AU$139.93.

## Products

- **Play Boosters and Collector Boosters**, both draftable in the usual premier-set way
- **Four preconstructed Commander decks**
- **Beam Me Up Bundle**
- **Scene Boxes**
- **Beginner Box**
- **Draft Night support** through the Wizards Play Network

The Beginner Box is worth flagging for anyone buying for a Star Trek fan who does not play Magic. Crossover sets attract exactly that audience, and a product designed as an entry point is unusual and useful.

## The signed cards

This is the element with no real precedent at this scale in a standard set release.

- Seven Star Trek actors individually hand-signed this set's headliner cards
- Each card was signed by the actor who originally portrayed the character
- Approximately **250 copies of each signed card**
- Randomly inserted into Collector Boosters

Seven cards at roughly 250 copies each is around 1,750 signed cards in total, distributed across the entire global print run of Collector Boosters. That is genuine scarcity, and it is worth being clear-eyed about what it means practically: your realistic chance of opening one is very small, and the sensible way to acquire one, if you want one, is on the secondary market after release rather than by opening product.

The actors have not been individually confirmed in the material we have verified, so we are not going to list names. When that is officially announced we will update this section.

## Mechanics

**New keywords:**
- **Assimilate**
- **Face a Dilemma**

**Returning from Edge of Eternities:**
- **Spacecraft**
- **Station**

The return of Spacecraft and station is the most informative detail for anyone trying to guess how this set will play. Edge of Eternities established those mechanics earlier in 2026, and reusing them here means Star Trek is building on an existing framework rather than introducing an entirely novel one. [Nova Hellkite](/cards/mtg/nova-hellkite) from Edge of Eternities is currently AU$1.93 if you want a cheap look at the space-set design language this is extending.

Assimilate is an obvious thematic fit for the Borg, and Face a Dilemma for Star Trek's habit of building episodes around moral choices. Neither keyword's full rules text has been verified by us, so we are describing the names and their evident thematic intent, not their mechanical effect.

## What the franchise coverage tells us

The named cards span the franchise rather than concentrating on one series. The Next Generation is represented by [Dr. Beverly Crusher](/cards/mtg/dr-beverly-crusher), the [U.S.S. Enterprise-D](/cards/mtg/u-s-s-enterprise-d-galaxy-class) and [Crystalline Entity](/cards/mtg/crystalline-entity). Voyager appears through [Seven of Nine](/cards/mtg/seven-of-nine). Deep Space Nine is represented by [In the Pale Moonlight](/cards/mtg/in-the-pale-moonlight), one of the most acclaimed episodes in the franchise. The Original Series contributes [The City on the Edge of Forever](/cards/mtg/the-city-on-the-edge-of-forever). Lower Decks brings [Beckett Mariner](/cards/mtg/beckett-mariner-impetuous-ensign) and [Brad Boimler](/cards/mtg/brad-boimler-eager-ensign).

Six series represented in nine priced cards is broad coverage, and it suggests a set built for the whole franchise audience rather than for Next Generation nostalgia alone. That matters commercially, because it widens the buyer pool considerably.

## Playability, honestly assessed

We cannot tell you how this set will play. Nine priced cards out of 135 tracked, two keywords whose rules text we have not verified, and a Standard format that has just been reset by the [August 2026 bans](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/) and is still settling.

What we can say is structural. Universes Beyond premier sets are Standard legal and draftable, so this is not a Commander-only product, and the return of Spacecraft and station means there is an existing mechanical framework for these cards to slot into rather than a from-scratch design.

Anyone publishing a Star Trek Standard tier list in August 2026 is guessing.

## The collector case, and the caution

**The case:** a 60th anniversary crossover, seven hand-signed cards at roughly 250 copies each, a fanbase far larger than Magic's own, a franchise with sixty years of accumulated attachment, and a full premier product line including collector-focused Scene Boxes.

**The caution:** three weeks ago we published [what happened to The Hobbit's preorder prices](/blog/the-hobbit-preorder-vs-post-release-prices/). Every one of that set's twenty most expensive cards fell after release. The basket lost 73.1 per cent. The Hobbit was also a Universes Beyond crossover with a large external fanbase, which is the closest available comparison to this set.

That does not mean Star Trek will behave identically. It does mean the pattern where crossover sets attract heavy preorder buying from outside the usual singles market, and then correct when supply arrives, is a documented one, and this set currently sits in exactly that window.

## Star Trek against the other late-2026 releases

Three major Magic releases land in the last quarter of 2026, and they are aimed at genuinely different buyers. Worth laying out, because the natural instinct is to treat them as competing for the same budget when mostly they are not.

| | Reality Fracture | Star Trek |
|---|---|---|
| Release | 2 October 2026 | 13 November 2026 |
| Type | Event set, in-universe | Universes Beyond crossover |
| Commander precons | One | Four |
| Headline hook | Echoed pairs mechanic | Hand-signed actor cards |
| Audience | Existing Magic players | Magic plus Star Trek fandom |

Reality Fracture closes a three year in-universe story arc, which makes it the set for players already invested in Magic's own narrative. Star Trek is the set that brings in people who may never have bought a Magic product before, which is exactly what a 60th anniversary crossover is designed to do.

The practical consequence for buyers is about timing rather than choosing. Reality Fracture's preorder window is open now and Star Trek's runs through October and into November, which means anyone buying both is making two separate preorder decisions six weeks apart, with the first one's post-release result available before the second one closes. That is an unusually useful position to be in, and it argues for watching what happens to Reality Fracture prices in early October before committing to Star Trek preorders.
