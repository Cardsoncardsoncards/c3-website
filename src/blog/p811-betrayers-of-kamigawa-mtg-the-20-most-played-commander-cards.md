Betrayers of Kamigawa is the second set in the Kamigawa block, released on the 4th of February, 2005. It's a smaller set than most, but it punched well above its weight in Commander circles thanks to a handful of cards that became staples in specific archetypes, particularly ninjas and legendary tribal strategies. This list ranks the twenty most played cards from the set in Commander, using EDHREC rank as the measure, with the lowest rank number indicating the most played card.

Every card here appears once on the list, even where a card had multiple printings within Betrayers of Kamigawa itself. Where that happened, the methodology takes the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across those printings, and prices the card by whichever printing carries the highest price. If two printings landed on exactly the same price, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved. As it happens, every card on this particular list had only a single priced printing within the set, so there's no tie-breaking to report here.

Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni sits at the top of the list, the single most played Betrayers of Kamigawa card in Commander decks according to EDHREC data, with an edhrec rank of 2825. From there the list runs down through cards with steadily climbing rank numbers and a real spread of prices, rarities and roles, from bombs to commons that found a home in ninja shells.

Prices quoted throughout are in Australian dollars and reflect a snapshot in time. Singles markets move, sometimes quickly, so treat every figure here as a point of reference rather than a guarantee of what you'll pay when you go looking for a copy.

**How does the ranking handle cards with multiple printings in the set?**
Each card appears once on the list, ranked by its best (lowest) EDHREC rank across its printings and priced by whichever printing carries the highest price. **What does EDHREC rank actually measure here?**
It reflects how often a card appears in Commander decks as tracked by EDHREC, with a lower rank number meaning the card is used more often relative to other cards.
