The Pokemon Company International has confirmed Pokemon TCG: First Partner Collection, Series 3, releasing as a new bundle box on 7 August 2026. It arrives in the middle of a packed 2026 for Pokemon TCG, a year already carrying the weight of the game's 30th anniversary.

## What the First Partner line has been about

The First Partner Collection series has built its identity around starter Pokemon, giving specific regional starter lines their own dedicated illustration-style products rather than folding them into a main set. Series 2 focused on starters from the Johto, Unova and Galar regions, released back in June. That pattern of pairing a bundle box with a tightly themed set of starters is the model Series 3 is expected to continue, though The Pokemon Company has not yet confirmed which regions this specific release covers.

## Why a bundle box format matters

Unlike a full booster set, a bundle box release is built around accessibility, typically combining packs with supplementary product in a single purchase rather than requiring a full case to chase specific pulls. That makes First Partner releases a reliably approachable entry point, whether you are buying for yourself or as a gift for someone newer to the game.

## Why this fits into Pokemon's anniversary year

2026 marks 30 years since the Pokemon TCG's original 1996 Japanese release, and the calendar reflects it, from the Mega Evolution era's main line expansions through to the all-foil 30th Celebration set arriving on 16 September as the first ever global same-day Pokemon TCG launch. First Partner Collection Series 3 fits into that broader anniversary rhythm as a smaller, starter-focused release rather than a headline anniversary product, but it is still part of the same year-long story, and worth collecting for that reason alone even if the featured regions turn out not to be your personal favourites.

## Why starter-focused releases keep working

Starter Pokemon carry a specific kind of nostalgia that outlasts any individual set's competitive relevance. A player's first partner Pokemon, whichever region they started in, tends to stay meaningful long after they have moved on from actively playing, which is exactly the audience a dedicated illustration-style product like this is built to reach. It is a different commercial logic to a main expansion chasing tournament relevance, and it explains why The Pokemon Company keeps returning to the format rather than folding starters into standard sets.

## How this compares to the main expansion line

Main Mega Evolution era sets like Pitch Black and the upcoming Storm Emerald carry the competitive weight of the format, introducing new Mega Evolution mechanics and tournament-relevant cards. First Partner releases sit deliberately apart from that, aimed at collectors and newer players rather than the competitive scene. Buying into both lines makes sense if your interest spans collecting and competitive play, but it is worth being clear with yourself about which category a purchase like Series 3 actually falls into before treating it as a competitive investment.
