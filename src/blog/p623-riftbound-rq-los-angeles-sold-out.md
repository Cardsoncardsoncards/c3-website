If you missed a badge for RQ Singapore, the natural next thought is that Los Angeles is your backup. It runs 25 to 27 September 2026 at the Los Angeles Convention Center, roughly three weeks after Singapore, and it is the next Regional Qualifier on the calendar. Unfortunately the premise does not survive contact with the facts. See current prices at [C3 Riftbound card prices](/cards/riftbound).

## Both events are sold out

RQ Los Angeles badges went on sale on 22 July 2026 and sold out. UVS Games' own Organized Play account has since opened a waitlist for the event, confirming that entry is no longer available through general sale.

That is the same pattern Singapore followed. Singapore's badges went on sale 8 July and are now showing as sold out on the official listing, also with a waitlist.

So the honest framing is not "Singapore is gone, book Los Angeles instead". It is that both APAC adjacent options on this stretch of the calendar are closed, and what remains in both cases is a waitlist.

## What a waitlist is actually worth

A waitlist is not a soft yes. UVS Games has been explicit that entry is not guaranteed and that waitlisted players are contacted only if seats become available.

For an Australian player this distinction carries real money. A waitlist place is not a reason to book flights or accommodation to Los Angeles. It is a reason to join the list, keep an eye on your email, and make no financial commitments that you cannot unwind if the call never comes.

Join both waitlists if you would genuinely attend either event. They cost nothing to join and they are the only remaining path to a seat.

It is worth knowing what you would be waiting on. According to Riftbound community tracker Runes & Rift, LA Premium badges include an exclusive Crystal Rose Sona, Harmonious playmat and a Vendetta gold-stamped Jayce, Brilliant Inventor promo. Treat that as community reporting rather than an official UVS Games confirmation, and check the official listing before you assume any particular badge tier carries it.

## The Los Angeles maths for an Australian player

Even if a Los Angeles seat did open up, it is a very different proposition to Singapore, and it is worth being clear eyed about that before you treat it as equivalent.

**Cost and distance.** Singapore is roughly 7 to 8 hours flying from the east coast with direct services from Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane. Los Angeles is a substantially longer and more expensive trip, and a late release seat means booking at short notice, which is when fares are at their worst.

**Eligibility.** Los Angeles is an Americas region event. Regional eligibility and qualification rules are set by UVS Games, not by us. Before spending anything on a Los Angeles trip, confirm directly with UVS Games that an Australian player can actually qualify through that event. Do not assume a Regional Qualifier invitation works the same way from every region.

**Timing.** This is the one genuine point in its favour. Los Angeles sits close enough after Singapore that the meta read coming out of Singapore will still be current. Three weeks is not long enough for the format to move much, so Singapore results transfer usefully.

## What to do instead

If neither waitlist comes through, the season is not over. The practical options are to keep grinding local Summoner Skirmish events and store level competition, and to watch the Singapore results closely when they land in early September.

That last point is worth more than it sounds. Singapore is the strongest APAC field of the year, and its results will shape the format going into the rest of the season. Watching them properly is free, and it is the best preparation available to anyone who is not in the room.
