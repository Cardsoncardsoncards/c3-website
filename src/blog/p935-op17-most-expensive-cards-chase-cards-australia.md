OP-17 The World's Strongest Warriors went to English wide release on 28 August 2026, four days ago. That is long enough for a singles market to form and far too short for it to have settled, so this article does two things: it ranks what is actually expensive right now, and it is explicit about how thin the ground under those numbers is. If you are buying in Australia this week, the second part matters more than the first.

**Data checked: 1 September 2026.** The set is four days into English release. Two dates matter for everything below and they are not the same one. **All card counts and all underlying market prices are as at the C3 catalogue sync of 31 August 2026**, which is the most recent sync at the time of writing. **Australian dollar figures are those prices converted at the rate the C3 Card Vault applied on 1 September 2026.** Every figure is a current asking price, not a completed sale. On a set this young the listing pools behind each price are small, and small pools move hard.

## Quick answer

The [Monkey.D.Luffy (079) Super Leader alternate art](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-monkeydluffy-079-super-leader-alternate-art-op17-079) is the most expensive card in OP-17 at **AU$3,625.00**, and the [Shanks (022) Manga rare](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-shanks-022-manga-op17-022) is second at **AU$2,066.25**. Nothing else in the set is close. Third place, the [Kaido (062) Super alternate art](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-kaido-062-super-alternate-art-op17-062) at **AU$521.35**, sits at roughly a seventh of the leader. Those top two cards alone are worth more than nine sealed booster boxes.

## How this ranking was built, and what it excludes

This matters more than usual here, because the obvious way to build this list produces a wrong answer.

OP-17 exists in the C3 Card Vault under **two** set names. This ranking covers the main booster set, **The World's Strongest Warriors**, and excludes the separate **The World's Strongest Warriors Release Event Cards**, which holds 76 further rows of event distribution promos whose top card is AU$71.57. Those are a different product with a different supply story.

**As at the catalogue sync of 31 August 2026**, the main set holds **183 rows**, and those rows are three different kinds of thing:

- **158 rows are genuine OP-17 numbered cards**, identified by an OP17 collector number in the card's own identifier. **153 of the 158 carry a price.**
- **19 rows are bundled promos that are not OP-17 cards at all**, identified by carrying a different set code in place of an OP17 one: ST31-004, EB04-007, OP13-028, OP16-098, ST27-005, OP14-108 and P-084. Several are expensive, and a naive sort would drop them into an OP-17 top ten where they do not belong.
- **6 rows are sealed product**, identified by carrying no card rarity at all. They include a booster box case at AU$6,202.38 that would otherwise rank first in a list of "most expensive cards" while not being a card.

**This ranking uses only the 158 OP-17 numbered cards, and only the 153 of them that carry a price.** Sealed product and promos from other sets are excluded. If you find an OP-17 value list elsewhere with a booster box case at the top, or with a Marshall.D.Teach ST27-005 inside it, that is the mistake it has made.

One caution on all counts here. **The set is four days old and its row count is still moving**, both as new printings are catalogued and as individual rows gain or lose a usable price. Every count in this article is dated to the 31 August 2026 sync for that reason, and a count taken a week from now will differ.

## Telling the treatments apart before you buy

Because treatment drives the price far more than rarity does, the practical risk for an Australian buyer is paying a treatment price for a standard card, or the reverse. Four labels appear in the top ten and they are not interchangeable.

**Standard printing** is the ordinary card. It is gameplay-legal, it is what comes out of most packs, and for every card in the table above it costs a small fraction of the treated version. If a listing photograph shows a normal bordered card, it is this, whatever the title says.

**Alternate art** replaces the artwork and keeps the frame. This is the most common premium treatment in OP-17 and the one most often mislabelled, because sellers frequently write "alt art" for any foil.

**Super alternate art** is the full-art treatment with the border removed. Kaido (062) and the top Luffy both use it, and the gap between the alternate art Luffy at AU$189.67 and the Super version at AU$3,625.00 is the single largest price step in the set. Confusing those two is the most expensive mistake available to you here.

**Manga rare** uses black-and-white manga panel artwork. Only Shanks (022) carries it in the top ten, and it is the second most valuable card in the set.

When a listing does not show the actual card front, assume the cheaper treatment and ask.

## Why the treatment matters more than the rarity

The set's own price distribution makes the point better than any argument could. Across the 153 priced OP-17 cards, **as at the 31 August 2026 sync**, the **average is US$37.78 and the median is US$0.42**. That gap is not a rounding artefact. Half the set is worth less than fifty US cents while a handful of cards drag the average up by a factor of ninety.

Broken down by rarity, across the same 153 priced cards on the same date, the pattern is clear:

Look at the Rare row. Its average is US$8.22, but its top card reaches US$111.20, and that card is the alternate art [Are You That Afraid of the New Era?!](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-are-you-that-afraid-of-the-new-era-alternate-art-op17-037). A Rare with a special treatment beats every Secret Rare in the set. The rarity letter on its own does not predict price here. Treatment does, and so does character.

Look also at Secret Rare, the rarity most buyers assume sits at the top. It averages US$63.98 and peaks at US$105.00. Both figures are beaten comfortably by ordinary Leaders carrying alternate art.

## The two-card problem

Cards one and two hold **AU$5,691.25** between them. The other eight cards in the top ten total **AU$1,631.53**. Two cards therefore carry more than three and a half times the remaining top eight combined, and the set's entire priced singles pool, all 153 of them at one copy each, sums to **AU$8,382.49** on the same 31 August 2026 figures.

For a buyer that concentration is the whole story. Value in OP-17 is not spread across a set you can gradually assemble. It sits in two printings. If you are not chasing those two, most of what you want is genuinely inexpensive. For a seller it cuts the other way: if you opened either card, the rest of your box barely affects what your box was worth.

If you are hunting either one specifically, the [current OP-17 Luffy listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=one+piece+op17+monkey+d+luffy+alternate+art&campid=5339146789&customid=op17-chase-luffy&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) and the [Shanks Manga rare listings](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=one+piece+op17+shanks+manga+rare&campid=5339146789&customid=op17-chase-shanks&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) are where Australian supply actually surfaces, since local shopfronts rarely list cards at this level.

## What this means if you are buying in Australia

**If you want the chase cards, wait.** Four days is close to the worst possible moment to pay a four-figure price. Release-week pricing on the two headline cards rests on very few listings, and the direction of travel on high-end One Piece alternate arts after the first fortnight has historically been downward rather than upward. Nothing in the current data suggests these two are exceptions.

**If you want to play the deck, buy standard printings now.** The gameplay-legal standard version of every card above is an ordinary card at an ordinary price, and the competitive cost of entering OP-17 is low. The median priced card in the set is forty-two US cents.

**If you are chasing a character rather than a specific card**, note that Luffy, Kaido and Rocks D. Xebec account for six of the top ten entries between them. Searching [OP-17 singles by character on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=one+piece+op17+worlds+strongest+warriors+singles&campid=5339146789&customid=op17-chase-singles&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) is more efficient than searching by rarity, because rarity is not what is driving these prices.

**If you are considering sealed**, the [booster box sits at AU$599.07](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-the-worlds-strongest-warriors-booster-box) and the case at AU$6,202.38. Whether either is worth opening is a separate question with a different method, and we have treated it separately in our [OP17 booster box versus singles analysis](/blog/op17-booster-box-vs-singles-australia/).

## What could move, and what we do not know

Three things are genuinely unsettled, and they are worth naming rather than implying the table above is stable.

**No competitive data exists yet.** As at 1 September 2026 there are no recorded OP-17 tournament results anywhere. The most recent events on the public tournament databases are from late July 2026 and are all OP-16. Every price above is therefore collector-led, not play-led. The first real results, including the Brisbane regional on 5 September 2026, could reprice playable cards sharply in either direction, and Rocks D. Xebec is the obvious candidate given that two of its printings already sit in the top ten on anticipation alone.

**The Japanese and English windows are unusually close.** Japan received OP-17 on 22 August and English wide release followed on 28 August. Earlier sets had a season between them, which let Japanese pricing act as a signal for English pricing. That signal is much weaker this time, and anyone quoting Japanese figures as a forecast for English cards should be read with caution.

**These are asking prices.** Nothing above is a confirmed sale. On cards with a handful of listings worldwide, the gap between what is asked and what actually clears can be very wide, and we have no completed-sale evidence at this price level yet.
