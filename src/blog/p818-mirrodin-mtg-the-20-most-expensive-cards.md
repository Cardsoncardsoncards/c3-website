Mirrodin arrived on 2 October 2003 and remains one of the most fondly remembered blocks in Magic's history, largely thanks to its artifact-heavy design and the mechanical identity it gave the plane for years afterward. This post looks at the twenty most expensive cards from the set, as priced in Australian dollars, and orders them purely by that headline figure. It is a snapshot of the secondary market rather than a guide to what any card does at the table, since we are only discussing price, printings and rarity here, nothing about how the cards play.

Where a card has more than one printing within Mirrodin itself, the ranking uses whichever printing carries the highest price, and any other printings are listed alongside it. If two printings happen to land on exactly the same price, that tie is noted plainly rather than quietly broken in one direction. As it happens, every card in this particular list has just a single priced printing within the set, so there are no ties or secondary printings to untangle this time round, but the methodology stands for any future set where that isn't the case.

Prices throughout are in Australian dollars and are, by nature, a moving target. Card markets shift with supply, reprints elsewhere, and general demand across the whole game, so treat every figure here as accurate at the time this snapshot was taken rather than a permanent number. For buying and selling in Australia, our own card pages carry current listings for these singles, and eBay Australia is a common secondary option for tracking down specific copies.

With that groundwork laid, here is how the twenty most expensive cards from Mirrodin stack up against one another, from the priciest at the top down to the twentieth entry rounding out the list.
