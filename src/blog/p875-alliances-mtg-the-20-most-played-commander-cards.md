Alliances arrived on 1996-06-10 as an early expansion to Magic's grey-bordered era, and it has quietly held onto a place in Commander decks decades later. This list ranks the twenty most played cards from the set in EDHREC's Commander data, ordered from number 1, the most played card, down to number 20, using EDHREC rank as the measure of play, where a lower number means the card turns up in more decks.

Several cards in Alliances were printed more than once within the set itself. Where that happens, the card is ranked by its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across those printings, and priced using whichever printing carries the higher price. Where two printings land on exactly the same price, that tie is called out rather than quietly smoothed over, so the figures stay honest to what is actually on the market.

Every price below is a snapshot in Australian dollars. Singles markets shift week to week, and a figure that holds today can drift within a month, so treat each price as a point in time rather than a fixed number. Readers looking to buy can check the card pages on our own site or search listings through eBay Australia, both of which will reflect more current pricing than any single article can.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Alliances release?**
Alliances released on 1996-06-10, making it one of the earlier expansion sets in Magic's history.

**Which card is the most played from Alliances in Commander?**
Arcane Denial holds that position, sitting at EDHREC rank 54, which is well ahead of every other card on this list.

**How does the ranking handle cards with more than one printing in the set?**
Each card is ranked using its best, or lowest, EDHREC rank across its printings within Alliances, and priced using its highest priced printing. Where two printings share exactly the same price, that tie is noted explicitly rather than resolved silently.

**Does a lower EDHREC rank mean more or less play?**
A lower EDHREC rank means the card appears in more Commander decks, so rank 54 for Arcane Denial reflects heavier play than a card ranked in the thousands.

**Are the prices in this list fixed?**

**Why do some lower-ranked cards cost more than higher-ranked ones?**
Play frequency and market price are measured independently in this data, so a card that appears in fewer decks can still carry a higher price than one played more often, as seen with Lake of the Dead compared to cards ranked above it.
