Pokemon 30th Celebration is the anniversary expansion, and it does something no Pokemon set has done before: it releases everywhere in the world on the same day, 16 September 2026. It also breaks the normal set structure in three ways at once. Every card in it is foil. Every pack contains one of thirty different Pikachu illustrations. And a new rarity, the Futuristic Rare, debuts on exactly two cards. This guide covers what is officially confirmed, what is still being revealed, and what the whole thing currently costs in Australia.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** This set has not released. Every price below is a pre-release asking price, not a completed sale, and pre-release pricing on anniversary products is historically volatile. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices, which convert market prices at the daily rate. Where the card list is incomplete, this guide says so rather than filling gaps from leaks.

## Quick answer

30th Celebration releases globally on 16 September 2026, the first Pokemon expansion ever to launch simultaneously worldwide. The main set is numbered to 128 cards, all foil. Each booster pack holds five foil cards, one of which is always one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations, plus a foil Basic Energy. Two cards, [Mewtwo ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mewtwo-ex-157-128) and [Mew ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mew-ex-158-128), carry the brand new Futuristic Rare treatment. A 30 card Classic Collection of reprints sits inside the release, with Base Set Charizard and the Pikachu and Zekrom Tag Team card among the confirmed inclusions. The Elite Trainer Box is currently AU$239.19 in pre-release pricing and the Pokemon Center version is AU$716.18.

## The set concept, and why it is structurally unusual

Most Pokemon sets are built around a chase tier sitting on top of a large body of ordinary cards. 30th Celebration inverts that. Making the entire set foil removes the usual distinction between a bulk card and a hit, which changes what opening a pack feels like and, more practically, changes what the bulk from this set is worth relative to a normal expansion.

The 30 Pikachu mechanic compounds it. Because one of the five foil cards in every pack is guaranteed to be a Pikachu, and because there are exactly thirty of them, the set has a built-in collecting objective that does not depend on rarity at all. You are not chasing one card thirty times. You are assembling a defined set of thirty, and the pack structure guarantees progress on every pack opened.

That is a deliberate design for an anniversary product, and it is worth understanding before you decide what to buy, because it makes sealed product behave differently from a normal set. We have written up the demand side of this separately in [why Pokemon 30th Celebration is so hyped](/blog/why-pokemon-30th-celebration-is-hyped/).

## The Futuristic Rare

The new rarity debuts on two cards only:

- [Mewtwo ex, 157/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mewtwo-ex-157-128), currently AU$1,450.00
- [Mew ex, 158/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mew-ex-158-128)

Two things to be careful about here. First, that AU$1,450 figure is a pre-release asking price on a card that does not exist in anyone's hands yet, derived from a thin listing pool. It is the single most volatile number in this guide and should not be treated as a valuation. Second, Mew ex currently has no recorded price at all, which is normal this far out and is a reminder that the market has not settled on how it values this new treatment.

Pairing Mewtwo and Mew for the debut of a new rarity in the 30th anniversary set is not an accident. They are the pair that anchored the original Base Set era chase, and putting the game's newest treatment on its oldest icons is the clearest single expression of what this set is doing.

## Collector questions worth answering now

**Is every card really foil?** Yes, that is confirmed as the set structure.

**Does that make bulk from this set worth more?** Probably somewhat, but do not over-read it. Universal foiling raises the floor of what a common looks like, it does not create demand for a common nobody wants.

**Should I buy the Ultra-Premium Collection?** Its contents are not yet fully confirmed, and there are two versions, Day at AU$588.77 and Night at AU$770.12. That is a large commitment against incomplete information. This is the product most worth waiting on.

**Will it be reprinted?** Unknown. No statement either way has been made. Anniversary sets attract buying pressure partly on an assumption of scarcity that nobody has actually confirmed, which is a real exposure rather than a certainty.

## Australian availability

The simultaneous global release is genuinely good news for Australian buyers, because it removes the usual window where overseas markets get first access and local supply arrives into already-inflated pricing. Everyone gets it on 16 September.

What it does not remove is allocation risk on the limited products. Pokemon Center exclusives and Ultra-Premium Collections are the products that historically sell out first here, and the pre-release pricing above already reflects that expectation.

Our recommendation is to price against the [C3 Pokemon Card Vault](/cards/pokemon) rather than against whatever a marketplace listing says this week, and to refresh before release, because pre-release pricing on a set three weeks out is the least reliable pricing that exists. ## What the simultaneous global release actually changes

This is the first Pokemon expansion to launch worldwide on one date, and it is worth being precise about what that does and does not fix.

**What it fixes.** Historically, staggered regional releases meant overseas markets set the price before Australian stock landed, so local buyers were frequently buying into a market that had already moved. That specific disadvantage disappears here.

**What it does not fix.** Simultaneous release synchronises demand as well as supply. Every market wants the same limited products in the same week rather than in sequence, which concentrates pressure on the constrained lines instead of spreading it. For the Elite Trainer Box and Booster Bundle that is unlikely to matter. For the Pokemon Center exclusives and the Ultra-Premium Collections it may matter a great deal.

We saw a version of this dynamic in One Piece a fortnight ago, where the game's first global simultaneous release compressed the buying window and Australian booster box listings ended up spread from AU$219.99 to AU$749.99 in the same week. The mechanism there is documented in [why OP17 got so expensive in Australia](/blog/why-op17-so-expensive-australia/), and the structural lesson carries across games even though the products do not.

## What to buy, by who you are

- **Opening for fun.** The Booster Bundle at AU$130.31 is the cheapest genuine entry to the pack experience, and with a guaranteed Pikachu per pack it delivers the set's core mechanic without the packaging premium.
- **Chasing the 30 Pikachu.** Packs, not boxed products. The Pikachu is pack-linked, so packaging tiers do not improve your rate.
- **Chasing the Futuristic Rares.** Do not buy sealed for this. Two cards in a 128 card set is not something to open into.
- **Playing competitively.** Wait. Nothing in this set is legal for the [World Championships](/blog/pokemon-worlds-2026-tcg-preview/) and its competitive impact will not be clear until well after release.
- **Sealed collecting.** The Pokemon Center ETB and the Ultra-Premium Collections are the products with genuine long-term scarcity arguments. They are also the ones with the most pre-release price risk.
