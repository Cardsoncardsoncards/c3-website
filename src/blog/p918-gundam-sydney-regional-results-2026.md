The Gundam Card Game Sydney Regional Championship was the largest Gundam event held in Australia to that point, and it produced a clear result: Barbatos Rush took three of the Top 8 slots including first place. This is the full breakdown of what won, what the field looked like, and how it reads against the current Freedom Ascension era.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** A correction first, because it matters. This article was commissioned on the understanding that Strike Freedom won a 207 player field. It did not. The verified result is a 230 player event won by Blue/Purple Barbatos Rush, with Blue/White Freedom Blockers finishing second. We have written the article to the evidence rather than to the brief, and we explain where that confusion likely comes from below.

## Quick answer

The Sydney Regional ran on Saturday 11 April 2026, organised by TAK Games, with 230 players across 8 rounds of Swiss in best-of-one, under an ST09-legal format with Corsica Base restricted. Lam won with Blue/Purple Barbatos Rush. Nikola finished second with Blue/White Freedom Blockers. Barbatos Rush placed first, third and fifth, making it comfortably the strongest performing archetype of the event.

Two notes on reading this data. First, it is a best-of-one Swiss event, which is a materially different test from best-of-three: it rewards consistent fast starts more and rewards sideboard-driven adaptation not at all. Aggressive archetypes tend to perform comparatively better in best-of-one, and that is worth holding in mind before treating this as a general statement about deck strength.

Second, this is an April event and today is late August. The format has moved since, most significantly with Freedom Ascension. This is a historical result, and we have not dressed it up as current.

## Archetype representation and what it shows

Three of eight Top 8 slots went to Barbatos Rush, including first, third and fifth. Two went to Freedom Blockers, at second and sixth. The remaining three slots were one each to Zeon Aggro, Dynames Rush and Justice Blockers.

The more interesting pattern is colour rather than archetype. **Seven of the eight Top 8 decks contained Blue.** Blue/Purple, Blue/White and Blue/Green account for every slot except the eighth place Red/White Justice Blockers.

That is a striking concentration, and it says more about the ST09-era format than the archetype counts do. A format where one colour appears in seven of eight top finishes is a format with a colour problem, not just a deck problem.

We do not have the full field breakdown, only the Top 8, so we cannot calculate conversion rates. Representation in the Top 8 and share of the 230 player field are different measures, and we are only able to report the first. Anyone quoting a conversion percentage for this event is extrapolating.

## Where the Strike Freedom confusion comes from

This is worth addressing directly, because the mix-up is easy to make and we nearly published it.

**Strike Freedom Gundam did not win this event.** It is, however, the single most prominent card in Gundam right now for two separate reasons.

First, [Strike Freedom Gundam](/cards/gundam/freedom-ascension-strike-freedom-gundam-lr-gd05-002-2179263) is the headline card of Freedom Ascension, the GD05 set, and its LR++ printing is currently AU$3,796.94. That is the most expensive Gundam card the [C3 Gundam Card Vault](/cards/gundam) tracks. The [LR+ printing](/cards/gundam/freedom-ascension-strike-freedom-gundam-lr-gd05-002) is a far more accessible AU$129.05, and the [Eternal Nexus EX version](/cards/gundam/eternal-nexus-strike-freedom-gundam-ex-lr-eb01-041) is AU$35.37.

Second, the archetype that finished second and sixth at Sydney is called **Freedom Blockers**, which is a name one word away from Strike Freedom and easy to conflate with it.

So: Freedom Blockers was the runner-up archetype at Sydney, and Strike Freedom is the headline chase card of a later set. Neither of those is "Strike Freedom won Sydney", and we are not going to print that it did.

## Cross-event comparison

Australia has now hosted several Gundam regionals, and Sydney's 230 players established the scale. We covered the [Brisbane regional separately](/blog/gundam-card-game-brisbane-regional-2026/), and the [July 2026 ban list](/blog/gundam-card-game-ban-list-july-2026/) changed the format after this event.

That ban list is the reason this Sydney result should not be read as current advice. A format with Corsica Base restricted and ST09 as the legality boundary is not the format Australian players are sitting in today, and any archetype ranking derived from April data needs re-testing against the current card pool before it means anything.

## What it means for the Freedom Ascension meta

The honest position is that it means less than it would have in April.

What survives from this result: Blue's dominance across seven of eight Top 8 slots is a structural observation about how the colour pie was performing, and structural observations age better than archetype rankings. Barbatos Rush proved itself as a genuine competitive shell rather than a hopeful one, and the Barbatos card pool has continued to grow since.

What does not survive: any claim about current tier placement. Freedom Ascension has landed since, bringing Strike Freedom with it, and the July ban list intervened between this event and now.

## What Barbatos Rush was actually doing

Worth explaining the archetype rather than just naming it, because "Rush" describes a plan and the plan is why it converted.

Barbatos Rush is built around the Barbatos line's escalating forms, which give the deck a natural curve of increasingly threatening units drawn from several sets rather than a single one. [Gundam Barbatos 1st Form](/cards/gundam/dual-impact-gundam-barbatos-1st-form-gd02-054) from Dual Impact anchors the early game, with [2nd Form](/cards/gundam/starter-deck-05-iron-bloom-gundam-barbatos-2nd-form-c-st05-002) and [4th Form](/cards/gundam/starter-deck-05-iron-bloom-gundam-barbatos-4th-form-lr-st05-001) from Starter Deck 05: Iron Bloom continuing it, and [Barbatos Lupus](/cards/gundam/steel-requiem-gundam-barbatos-lupus-lr-gd03-050) from Steel Requiem at the top end.

That structure is exactly what best-of-one Swiss rewards. A deck with a consistent, escalating curve does not need to adapt between games because there are no games between which to adapt. It needs to execute the same plan eight times, and it needs to be difficult to interact with on the way up.

Freedom Blockers, the runner-up archetype, is the structural opposite: a control plan built on blocking and attrition. That it took second and sixth in a best-of-one field is arguably the more impressive result, because the format was tilted against it.

The cheap entry point here is worth noting for anyone testing the archetype. The Barbatos line runs through Starter Deck 05, and the [2nd Form at AU$14.01](/cards/gundam/starter-deck-05-iron-bloom-gundam-barbatos-2nd-form-c-st05-002) shows how accessible the core is once you set aside the premium printings.
