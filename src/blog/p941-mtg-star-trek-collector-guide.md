Magic: The Gathering | Star Trek releases on 13 November 2026, and it is the most treatment-heavy Universes Beyond set Wizards has produced. Five distinct card treatments, all ten shock lands reprinted in a bespoke frame, and seven hand-signed cards at roughly 250 copies each. This guide covers what is officially confirmed about the collector side, what is likely to matter, and what Australian buyers should be careful about.

**Data checked: 1 September 2026.** The set has not released. Everything below comes from official Wizards of the Coast collector information published ahead of release. **No prices are quoted for the set's cards**, because there is no post-release market to quote and preorder figures on an unreleased Universes Beyond set are not evidence of anything. Product prices are the official US figures as published.

## Quick answer

The chase of this set is the **signed headliner cards: seven different cards, hand-signed by Star Trek actors, at approximately 250 copies of each**. That is roughly 1,750 signed cards in total across the entire print run, which makes them the scarcest thing Wizards has confirmed for the set. Named examples include [Captain James T. Kirk](/cards/mtg/captain-james-t-kirk) and [Captain Kathryn Janeway](/cards/mtg/captain-kathryn-janeway).

Below that, the **LCARS shock lands** are the treatment most likely to hold broad value, because all ten are included and shock lands have durable demand independent of the crossover.

## The five treatments, explained

Understanding these is the whole collector story, because the same card can appear in several of them at wildly different scarcities.

### Signed headliners

**Seven different cards, approximately 250 copies of each, hand-signed by Star Trek actors.** This is the top of the set and it is not close. At 250 copies per card these are serial-number-scarce without being serialised, and the signatures tie each one to a specific actor rather than to the card alone. Kirk and Janeway are confirmed among them.

Two things to be careful about here. First, only seven cards carry this treatment, so most "signed" listings you see after release will be either one of those seven or something signed privately at a convention, which is a completely different product. Second, Wizards has confirmed the approximate count but the practical availability in Australia will be far below a proportional share, for reasons covered further down.

### Stardates

**Thirty different reprints given Star Trek artwork.** These recast existing Magic cards as Star Trek characters, and the published example is Sheoldred depicted as "Khan, Engineered Evil". This is the treatment most likely to produce a surprise chase, because a Stardates version of an already-expensive Magic staple combines two demand pools: Star Trek collectors and players who want the card anyway.

Their own set code, SDS, indicates they are tracked separately from the main set.

### LCARS frame

**Twenty-nine cards using the Enterprise computer interface design.** LCARS is the in-universe display system from The Next Generation, and the frame is the most visually distinctive thing in the set. This is the treatment that will look most obviously "Star Trek" on a table.

### LCARS shock lands

**All ten shock lands, in the LCARS frame, depicting planetary landscapes.** This is the single most commercially significant inclusion in the set for players.

Shock lands are format staples in Modern, Pioneer and Commander. A complete cycle in a desirable bespoke frame is the kind of thing that sells a set to people with no interest in the licence, and it means Play and Collector Boosters have a floor of value independent of whether Star Trek appeals to the buyer. Cards like [Blood Crypt](/cards/mtg/blood-crypt) will be the most-opened chase in the set.

### Classic Enterprise

**Twenty-four cards in a retro painterly aesthetic** honouring the original series posters. This is the nostalgia treatment, and it targets the sixty-year anniversary directly.

### Scene cards

**Twenty-five borderless cards depicting iconic Star Trek moments across multiple cards.** The mechanic here is that a single scene spans more than one card, so completing a scene requires acquiring the full group. That is a deliberate collector hook and it tends to produce uneven pricing, where the least-opened card in a scene carries a premium well above its individual desirability.

Scene Boxes at US$41.99 exist as a separate product for exactly this reason.

## The four Commander decks

| Deck | Colours | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Federation Fleet | Jeskai | Starships and card draw |
| Landing Party | Bant | Landfall and away-team exploration |
| Klingon Fury | Mardu | Aggressive Warrior combat |
| We Are the Borg | Esper | Artifact creatures, with the new Assimilate mechanic |

**We Are the Borg carries the set's new mechanic, Assimilate.** A precon carrying a brand new named mechanic usually attracts the most attention of a Commander cycle, and the Borg are the most mechanically distinctive faction in the licence. [Borg Queen, Perfection Manifest](/cards/mtg/borg-queen-perfection-manifest) is the deck's headline card.

The Collector's Edition versions at US$159.99 are surge foil throughout, which is more than double the standard price. Whether that is worth it depends entirely on whether the deck's chase cards hold value in foil, and that is not knowable before release.

## Characters and ships to watch

C3 already lists card pages for a substantial part of the set. Among the confirmed cards worth watching from a collector angle:

- [Captain James T. Kirk](/cards/mtg/captain-james-t-kirk) and [Captain Kathryn Janeway](/cards/mtg/captain-kathryn-janeway), both mythic and both confirmed signed headliners
- [Captain Kirk, Boldly Going](/cards/mtg/captain-kirk-boldly-going), the Commander-set version
- [Borg Queen, Perfection Manifest](/cards/mtg/borg-queen-perfection-manifest) and [Benjamin Sisko, Besieged](/cards/mtg/benjamin-sisko-besieged), both mythic Commander cards
- [U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class](/cards/mtg/u-s-s-enterprise-d-galaxy-class), the headline ship
- [Crystalline Entity](/cards/mtg/crystalline-entity), a mythic and one of the few cards already carrying an early preorder figure
- [Dr. Beverly Crusher](/cards/mtg/dr-beverly-crusher) and [Seven of Nine](/cards/mtg/seven-of-nine)
- [The City on the Edge of Forever](/cards/mtg/the-city-on-the-edge-of-forever), [Amok Time](/cards/mtg/amok-time), [In the Pale Moonlight](/cards/mtg/in-the-pale-moonlight) and [Dathon and Picard at El-Adrel](/cards/mtg/dathon-and-picard-at-el-adrel), all named after landmark episodes

That last group is worth a note. Episode-titled cards tend to attract collectors who are not Magic players at all, and "The City on the Edge of Forever" and "Amok Time" are two of the most celebrated episodes in the franchise. Cards named after them have a demand source outside the game.

## Chase candidates, and why we are not ranking them

A collector guide for an unreleased set that publishes a ranked chase list is guessing, so here is the reasoning instead of a table.

**Most likely to be genuinely scarce:** the seven signed headliners, at roughly 250 copies each. Scarcity here is confirmed rather than inferred.

**Most likely to hold value long-term:** the LCARS shock lands. Ten format staples in a bespoke frame have demand that outlasts the crossover's novelty.

**Most likely to surprise:** a Stardates reprint of an expensive staple. Combining an existing high-value card with a scarce treatment is historically how Universes Beyond sets produce their biggest single-card prices.

**Most likely to disappoint:** the Classic Enterprise treatment. Retro aesthetics are divisive, twenty-four cards is a lot of them, and nostalgia treatments have a mixed record at holding early pricing.

**Unknowable right now:** everything about actual prices. There is no market, and preorder figures on Universes Beyond sets have a poor record of predicting post-release value in either direction.

## Australian availability and risks

**Expect a wider gap than usual between US and Australian pricing.** The US figures above are official. Australian retail on Universes Beyond product routinely lands well above the converted US price, and a Collector Booster at US$37.99 is unlikely to convert cleanly to Australian shelves.

**Signed card availability will be disproportionately low here.** Roughly 1,750 signed cards exist across the global print run. Australia's share of global Magic distribution is small, and high-value singles historically flow toward the United States market rather than away from it. If you want one, expect to buy it internationally.

**Universes Beyond sets carry a specific reprint risk.** Wizards has reprinted popular Universes Beyond cards in later products before, and the shock lands in particular exist in many frames already. A bespoke frame protects a card's collector premium only as long as the frame stays exclusive.

**Prerelease timing is the practical Australian risk.** Prerelease runs 6 to 12 November with release on 13 November. Australian stores frequently receive Universes Beyond allocations later than the northern hemisphere, so preordering with a specific local store is usually more reliable than expecting release-day shelf stock.

## Which product to buy for which goal

The product range is wide enough that the right purchase depends heavily on what you actually want, and the wrong one is expensive.

**If you want the shock lands:** Collector Boosters at US$37.99. The LCARS shock lands are the treatment most concentrated in that product, and buying the lands as singles after release is usually cheaper than opening for them, so only do this if you enjoy opening.

**If you want to play Commander:** the standard decks at US$74.99. The Collector's Edition at US$159.99 is a surge foil upgrade, not a gameplay one, and it costs more than double for the same seventy-five cards.

**If you want the new mechanic:** We Are the Borg, the Esper deck carrying Assimilate. New named mechanics arrive in precons more often than they arrive in boosters, and this is the one.

**If you want the scenes complete:** Scene Boxes at US$41.99. Chasing multi-card scenes through boosters is the least efficient path in the set, because you need specific cards rather than any good card.

**If you want a signed headliner:** none of the above reliably. Seven cards at roughly 250 copies each will not come out of a sensible number of boosters. Budget to buy the specific card as a single, from an international seller, and accept that the price will be set by a very small market.

**If you want to open something fun:** the Beam Me Up Bundle at US$99.99, which includes two of eight promo spacecraft cards. Bundles are usually the weakest value per card in a set and the best value per hour of enjoyment.

## What we cannot tell you yet

Three things are genuinely unknown as at 1 September 2026, and the set is more than two months from release.

**Pull rates.** Wizards has not published per-slot odds for the signed cards or the individual treatments. Anyone quoting a chance of opening a signed headliner is estimating.

**Prices.** There is no post-release market. C3 carries early figures on a handful of cards, and those are preorder asking prices on an unreleased product, which is the least reliable kind of pricing there is.

**The full card list.** Spoiler season is not complete, so the twenty-nine LCARS cards, twenty-five scene cards and thirty Stardates reprints are not all publicly known.

We will revisit this with actual pricing once the set is out and a real singles market exists.
