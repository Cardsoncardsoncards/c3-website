Vendetta released on 31 July 2026, which means there is now about a week of real tournament data to work from instead of preview season speculation. That is a small sample, and it is worth saying so up front. But it is enough to check whether the pre release predictions are holding, and the early answer is more interesting than most people expected. ## What the early results actually show

Irelia, Blade Dancer has taken the largest share of early Top 8 finishes, with roughly a tenth of the recorded metashare. Behind it, Kennen, Heart of the Tempest, Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman and Ezreal, Prodigal Explorer are all posting results.

Early community tier lists broadly agree with the tournament data rather than contradicting it, placing Irelia, Ezreal and Kennen at the top. When the theorycrafting and the actual results point the same way this early, that is usually a sign the read is reasonable, though it can also mean the field is still copying the same preview season lists as each other.

## Vendetta has not displaced Unleashed

This is the finding that matters most, and it is the one that gets buried under legend tier lists.

Vendetta is struggling to deeply impact the metagame so far. A handful of Vendetta legends are posting results, but Unleashed still wins most tournaments. A new set landing does not automatically reset the format, and one week in, it has not.

For anyone preparing for a competitive event, that reframes the decision. The instinct after a set release is to assume your Unleashed deck is now obsolete and to rebuild around the new cards. The early data does not support that. A refined Unleashed deck is not the liability it might have looked like in May.

## What this means if you are preparing for RQ Singapore

RQ Singapore runs 4 to 6 September 2026, which leaves several more weeks of results before the event. If you have been playing an Unleashed legend seriously since the season began, the case for abandoning it is weaker than the release hype suggests.

The players who tend to do well in an unsettled format are usually the ones with the deepest understanding of one deck's decision trees, not the ones who switched to the newest legend a fortnight before the event. Depth of practice beats novelty when the format has not resolved.

That said, this cuts both ways. If Vendetta legends climb over the next month, arriving with a deck the field has already solved is its own problem. The useful approach is to keep tracking results weekly rather than locking a decision now based on week one.

## Treat this as provisional

One week of results is a small sample. Early metagames are noisy, they overrepresent whoever built fastest, and they routinely look nothing like the format three months later.

Nothing here should be read as a settled tier list. It is a snapshot of a format that is still moving, and the sensible use of it is to inform what you test next, not to decide what you sleeve up in September.
