The Pokemon Company International has confirmed Mega Forces Tins for release on 28 August 2026, a three-tin lineup that gives collectors a focused, single-Pokemon entry point into the ongoing Mega Evolution era rather than a full booster set commitment.

## What is actually in each tin

Three tins are confirmed, each built around a different Mega Evolution, Mega Dragonite ex, Mega Darkrai ex and Mega Zeraora ex. Every tin ships with a brand new foil card featuring that specific Pokemon on the exterior artwork, giving each one a clear individual identity rather than a shared generic design across the range. That per-Pokemon distinctiveness is a deliberate departure from some earlier tin waves, which leaned more heavily on shared artwork templates across an entire release.

## Why tin releases matter in the Mega Evolution era

2026's Mega Evolution era has been built around reintroducing Mega Evolution as a core mechanic, following on from Pokemon Legends: Z-A, and the era has already delivered a run of main expansions through the year, Ascended Heroes, Perfect Order, Chaos Rising and Pitch Black among them. A tin release like Mega Forces sits alongside those main sets as a lower-commitment way to pick up a specific Mega Evolution you care about, without needing to chase it through booster packs from a full expansion. That makes it a genuinely different kind of purchase decision to a booster box, one built around certainty rather than the thrill of the chase.

## Which tin to prioritise

That depends entirely on which of the three Pokemon you actually want. Dragonite, Darkrai and Zeraora each carry a genuinely different fanbase, and unlike a booster box where you are gambling on pulling a specific card, a tin release guarantees the exact exclusive foil tied to that Pokemon. If your interest is collector-driven rather than competitive, this is about as close to a sure thing as a TCG release gets. Buying all three is a simple way to cover your bases if budget allows, since none of the three tins carries a materially different price point to the others based on what has been confirmed so far.

## Why tins remain popular despite smaller contents

A tin release will never match a booster box for sheer volume of cards, and that is precisely the point rather than a downside. Tins are built for a buyer who knows exactly what they want going in, rather than one hoping to pull it from a larger case. That certainty is worth a premium to plenty of collectors, particularly for a Pokemon as specific as one of these three Mega Evolutions, where opening pack after pack from a full box with no guarantee is a genuinely different experience to knowing the exclusive foil is already secured inside the box.

## What each Pokemon brings to the lineup

Dragonite has long carried a dedicated following as one of the original 151, and a Mega Evolution treatment of it taps directly into that nostalgia. Darkrai's darker, more mysterious design has made it a consistent collector favourite since its introduction, and a Mega treatment plays well to that reputation. Zeraora, a comparatively newer addition to the Pokemon roster, gives the lineup a more contemporary pull that may appeal to collectors who came to the game more recently than the Dragonite and Darkrai fanbases typically did.

## Where this sits in the broader 2026 calendar

Mega Forces Tins land between First Partner Collection Series 3 on 7 August and the milestone 30th Celebration set on 16 September, making late August a comparatively quieter release window sandwiched between two bigger moments in Pokemon's anniversary year. That relative quiet is arguably part of the appeal, giving each of the three tins room to be judged on its own merits rather than competing directly with a bigger, more heavily marketed release for attention.
