Pokemon 30th Celebration has generated more pre-release attention than any Pokemon set in years, and it releases on 16 September 2026. Some of that attention is grounded in genuinely unusual confirmed product decisions. Some of it is people extrapolating from an assumption nobody has verified. This article separates the two, because the difference decides whether you should buy now or wait.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The set has not released. Every price here is a pre-release asking price on a product that does not exist in anyone's hands, which is the least reliable pricing in the hobby. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

The real drivers are three confirmed product decisions: every card in the set is foil, every pack guarantees one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations, and a brand new rarity called the Futuristic Rare debuts on exactly two cards. Those are genuinely unusual and they justify real interest. The fourth driver, the widespread assumption that 30th Celebration will be scarce and will not be reprinted, has no confirmation behind it at all. That assumption is doing a great deal of work in current pricing, and it is the part you should be sceptical about.

## Driver one: the anniversary itself, and what it actually means

Thirty years is a real milestone and The Pokemon Company has treated it as one. But anniversary status by itself is not a reason to expect value. Anniversary sets get more attention, more products and more marketing. Whether they get more scarcity is a separate question that nobody has answered here.

What is genuinely notable is the release structure. This is the first Pokemon expansion in the game's history to launch simultaneously worldwide, on 16 September 2026, followed by further product waves on 2 October, 30 October, 6 November and 4 December 2026.

That five wave rollout is worth thinking about carefully, because it cuts against the scarcity narrative rather than supporting it. A product line spread across nearly three months is not the shape of a constrained release.

## Driver two: every card is foil

This is confirmed and it is structurally unusual.

In a normal Pokemon set, most cards are non-foil commons that are worth close to nothing, and the foil treatment is part of what marks a card as a hit. 30th Celebration removes that distinction entirely. Every card in the set is foil.

**What this genuinely changes:** the floor. Bulk from this set looks different from bulk from a normal set, and opening a pack feels different when there is no filler.

**What it does not change:** demand. A foil common that nobody wants is still a common that nobody wants. Universal foiling raises presentation across the board, and when a treatment applies to everything it stops being a differentiator within the set. Anyone telling you this makes 30th Celebration bulk valuable is skipping that step.

## Driver three: the 30 Pikachu mechanic

Each booster pack contains five foil cards, one of which is always one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations, plus one foil Basic Energy.

This is the smartest thing in the set from a demand perspective, and it is worth understanding why. It creates a bounded, achievable collecting objective that does not depend on rarity. You are not chasing one card repeatedly against long odds. You are assembling a defined set of thirty, and every single pack advances you toward it.

That structure produces sustained buying rather than lottery buying, and it is the driver most likely to keep demand alive well past release week. Twenty of the thirty are currently loaded on the [C3 Pokemon Card Vault](/cards/pokemon), and we are tracking the full checklist in [all 30 Pikachu cards in 30th Celebration](/blog/pokemon-30th-celebration-30-pikachu-cards/).

## Driver four: the Futuristic Rare

A brand new rarity debuting on exactly two cards:

- [Mewtwo ex, 157/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mewtwo-ex-157-128), currently AU$1,450.00
- [Mew ex, 158/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mew-ex-158-128), no recorded price

Putting the newest treatment in the game's history on Mewtwo and Mew, the pair that anchored the original Base Set chase, is a deliberate and effective piece of design. This is the clearest single expression of what the set is doing.

It is also where the most speculative money is. That AU$1,450 figure is a pre-release asking price derived from a thin listing pool on a card nobody has held. Its counterpart has no price at all, which tells you the market has not settled on how to value the treatment. Two cards in a 128 card set is a very small target, and buying sealed product hoping to hit it is not a plan.

## Driver five: the Classic Collection, and the nostalgia question

A 30 card Classic Collection of reprinted classics sits inside the release. Officially confirmed inclusions: Base Set Charizard, and the Pikachu and Zekrom Tag Team card.

That is two out of thirty. The other 28 are unannounced.

There is a great deal of confident speculation circulating about what the remaining 28 are, and it is worth being blunt: none of it is confirmation. The [Classic Collection Pack](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-classic-collection-pack) is currently listed at AU$361.04 in pre-release pricing, which is a substantial commitment against a product whose contents are 93 per cent unknown.

Base Set Charizard is genuinely the strongest nostalgia lever available in this hobby. That is real. Paying AU$361 in advance for a pack where you know two of thirty cards is a different proposition.

## The Eeveelution factor

The set leans heavily on Eeveelutions, which have their own dedicated collector base largely independent of competitive play. Confirmed in the main set:

- [Espeon ex, 070/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-espeon-ex-070-128)
- [Umbreon ex, 092/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-umbreon-ex-092-128)
- [Sylveon ex, 071/128](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-sylveon-ex-071-128)
- Plus plain [Espeon](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-espeon-069-128), [Umbreon](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-umbreon-091-128) and two [Eevee](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-eevee-117128-117-128)

Espeon and Umbreon in particular anchor the Ultra-Premium Collection product line. Umbreon has a long history of driving Pokemon collector demand harder than almost any other Pokemon, and its presence here is not accidental.

## What is real hype and what is not

**Real, because it is confirmed product fact:**
- Every card is foil
- Guaranteed Pikachu per pack, thirty to collect
- New Futuristic Rare on Mewtwo and Mew
- Base Set Charizard reprint confirmed
- Heavy Eeveelution presence
- First simultaneous global release

**Not established, and currently priced as if it were:**
- That the set will be scarce. No statement has been made about print runs.
- That it will not be reprinted. No statement has been made either way, and the five wave rollout points the other direction.
- That the Classic Collection contains any specific card beyond the two confirmed.
- That the Futuristic Rares will hold AU$1,450. That is a pre-release asking price with essentially no transaction history behind it.
- That sealed product is an investment. This is the assumption underneath most of the current pricing and it is the one with the least support.

## The precedent worth knowing

Three weeks ago we published [the 20 most expensive Hobbit cards](/blog/the-hobbit-top-20-most-expensive-cards/) at preorder prices. We [went back and checked](/blog/the-hobbit-preorder-vs-post-release-prices/) after release. Every one of the twenty fell. The basket lost 73.1 per cent in three weeks and the best performer still lost 38.1 per cent.

Different game, different company, different product. But the mechanism is identical, and it is not game specific: pre-release prices are set by a small number of listings against demand that has nothing else to buy, and they discover a real level once supply exists.

Pokemon 30th Celebration currently sits entirely in that pre-supply window.

## What this means in Australia

The simultaneous global release genuinely helps Australian buyers. Historically, staggered releases meant overseas markets set prices before local stock landed. That specific disadvantage is gone.

What remains is allocation risk on the limited lines. The [Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-pokemon-center-elite-trainer-box) at AU$716.18 against the [standard Elite Trainer Box](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-elite-trainer-box) at AU$239.19 is a three times premium, and that gap already reflects the market pricing in an expectation of scarcity that has not been confirmed.

The practical advice is unglamorous. If you want to open packs and chase Pikachu, the [Booster Bundle](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-booster-bundle) at AU$130.31 delivers the set's actual mechanic without the packaging premium. If you want the exclusives, understand you are paying for an assumption. Price everything against the [C3 Pokemon Card Vault](/cards/pokemon) rather than against a marketplace listing, and [compare current 30th Celebration listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=pokemon+30th+celebration+elite+trainer+box&campid=5339146789&customid=pokemon-30th-hype&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) before committing to a pre-order.

## The C3 take

The hype is more justified than usual. Three confirmed design decisions in this set are genuinely unusual, and the Pikachu mechanic in particular is well built to sustain interest past release week.

The pricing is less justified than the hype. Almost every current figure rests on a scarcity assumption that nobody has confirmed and that the five wave release schedule actively undercuts. Wait for supply on the expensive products, and buy the cheap ones if you want to enjoy the set.
