The Pokemon TCG 30th Celebration Ultra-Premium Collection comes in two versions, Day with Espeon and Night with Umbreon, and they are not priced the same. Night currently trades meaningfully higher. This article covers what is actually in the box, what the two versions cost in Australia right now, and the one number that matters most for Australian buyers: the gap between what local retailers are asking on preorder and what the secondary market has already moved to.

**Data checked: 1 September 2026.** The 30th Celebration set releases on 16 September 2026 and the Ultra-Premium Collections are listed by Australian retailers for a 6 November 2026 window. Nothing here has been opened or tested by C3. This is an analysis of official product contents and live pricing, not a review. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices unless attributed to a named retailer, and they are current asking prices rather than completed sales.

## Quick answer

**Night (Umbreon) is the more expensive of the two**, at **AU$850.34** against **AU$776.98** for Day (Espeon), a gap of AU$73.36. **But both market figures sit far above Australian retail preorder pricing**, which is around **AU$449.99** at Australian specialist retailers. If you want one of these, the preorder is the transaction that matters, and it is currently the single largest saving available on any 30th Celebration product.

Whether the box is "worth it" depends entirely on which of those two numbers you pay.

## What is officially in the box

Both versions carry the same structure. Only the Eeveelution changes.

| Contents | Detail |
|---|---|
| Booster packs | 29 x 30th Celebration boosters |
| Bonus pack | 1 x Classic Collection booster containing 3 etched cards |
| Promo cards | Foil Pikachu ex promo, plus foil Espeon ex (Day) or Umbreon ex (Night) |
| Playmat | Themed to Pikachu with Espeon or Umbreon |
| Sleeves | 65 card sleeves |
| Accessories | Deck box, metal coin featuring Espeon or Umbreon, 6 damage-counter dice |
| Digital | Pokemon TCG Live code card |

**Thirty packs is the headline number**, and it is what separates this from every other 30th Celebration product. The Classic Collection booster is the genuinely distinctive inclusion: three etched nostalgic cards that do not appear in the standard boosters.

The set itself is unusual in ways that make the pack count matter more than normal. Every card in 30th Celebration is foil, including Basic Energy. Each pack contains one of 30 unique Pikachu rare cards, guaranteed. A new rarity, Futuristic Rare (FUR), debuts in the set. And it is the first Pokemon expansion to release simultaneously worldwide.

**The direction reverses depending on the product.** Umbreon commands the premium on the expensive box. Espeon commands it on both cheaper products. That is not what you would expect if one Eeveelution were simply more popular than the other.

The most plausible reading, and it is inference rather than established fact, is that the two are drawing on different buyer pools. Umbreon has the stronger track record as a high-end collector chase in Pokemon, which is where a AU$850 box competes. At AU$50 to AU$115 the buyer is a player or a casual collector, and that pool appears to split more evenly, with Espeon slightly ahead. We would not build a purchase decision on that theory, but the price pattern is real and checkable.

**If you simply want the cheaper of the two big boxes, that is Day with Espeon**, at a AU$73.36 saving today.

## The number that actually matters for Australian buyers

Set the Espeon and Umbreon question aside for a moment, because there is a much larger gap in play.

| Price point | Figure |
|---|---:|
| United States MSRP | US$179.99 |
| Australian retailer preorder | around **AU$449.99** |
| C3 Card Vault market, Day | AU$776.98 |
| C3 Card Vault market, Night | **AU$850.34** |

At the current conversion rate the US MSRP is roughly AU$261. **Australian preorder pricing is therefore already around seventy per cent above US MSRP converted**, which is a wider gap than Australian Pokemon buyers usually face and worth knowing before you decide this is a bargain.

**But the secondary market is higher again.** The Night box is trading at nearly double the Australian preorder price. If Australian retail preorders at AU$449.99 hold and are honoured, that is a substantial gap in the buyer's favour, and it is by far the strongest argument for buying this product.

Two cautions on that. First, preorder pricing on high-demand Pokemon products is frequently revised or cancelled, so an advertised preorder is not a secured purchase. Second, market pricing on a product six weeks from release rests on thin listing pools and preorder speculation rather than settled trade, and it can fall as sharply as it rose once supply lands.

**A note on the case.** At AU$3,067.91 the sealed case works out slightly below the sum of its individual boxes at current market pricing, which is the usual relationship. It is a reseller purchase rather than a collector one.

**None of these carries a live price yet**, because the set has not released. That is the honest position: we can tell you what is in the box and what the box costs, but we cannot tell you what the promos inside it will be worth, and anyone who claims otherwise is guessing. Once the set is out and the singles market forms, the value question becomes answerable and we will revisit it.

## Sealed versus opening

If you do buy one, the second decision is whether to open it, and the two versions of that decision are not equally reversible.

**Opening destroys the sealed premium permanently.** Ultra-Premium Collections are among the products most likely to hold or gain value sealed, because the configuration is not reprinted and the box itself becomes the collectible. Once opened you own thirty packs of cards and a playmat, and the box is worth a fraction of what it was.

**But the sealed case is weaker than usual here.** 30th Celebration is a worldwide simultaneous release, which is unusual and which generally means larger initial print runs than a staggered regional release produces. Larger supply is the enemy of sealed appreciation. A product that is easy to obtain in November is unlikely to be scarce in February.

**The opening case rests on one unknown.** Thirty packs of a set where every card is foil, every pack contains a guaranteed Pikachu rare from a pool of thirty, and a brand new Futuristic Rare tier exists, is a genuinely unusual opening experience. Whether it is a financially good one depends on what the FUR cards and the Special Illustration Rares settle at, and nobody knows that yet because the set is two weeks from release.

**Our position:** if you are buying at Australian preorder pricing near AU$449.99 the decision can wait, because you are well under market either way. If you are paying AU$776.98 or more, opening is difficult to justify, because you are paying a large premium for packs whose contents have no established value.

## Who should buy, and who should skip

**Buy if you can secure an Australian preorder near AU$449.99.** At that price you are buying roughly forty-seven per cent below the current market figure for the Night box. That is the whole case, and it is a good one.

**Buy if you want the Classic Collection booster and the accessories.** The etched cards and the playmat, coin and sleeves are not available in this configuration elsewhere, and if those are what you want then the per-pack maths is beside the point.

**Skip if you are paying market price.** At AU$776.98 to AU$850.34 you are paying a large premium over an Elite Trainer Box for thirty packs of a set whose singles have no established value, six weeks before release. That is speculation on an unopened product.

**Skip if you only want the cards.** Thirty packs is a lot of sealed product to buy for promos you could acquire as singles after release, usually for less.

**If you want Umbreon specifically**, be aware you are paying AU$73.36 extra today for that preference at the top of the range, and that the premium runs the other way on the cheaper products.
