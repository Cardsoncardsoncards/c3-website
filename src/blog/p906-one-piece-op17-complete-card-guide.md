OP-17 The World's Strongest Warriors is the One Piece Card Game's 4th anniversary set, and it is the first main booster in the game's history to launch in Japanese and English within a week of each other rather than a season apart. Japan got it on 22 August 2026. The English pre-release ran 21 August, and English wide release lands 28 August 2026. For Australian players that compressed window changes the buying calculus more than any single card in the set does, and this guide covers both halves: what is actually in OP-17, and what it is worth here right now.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** Australian dollar figures below are the live C3 Card Vault prices on that date. C3 prices are TCGplayer market prices converted to Australian dollars at the daily rate, so they track the US market and will not always match an Australian shopfront's asking price. Sealed prices quoted from Australian retailers are asking prices, not completed sales.

## Quick answer

OP-17 contains 131+4 card types by Bandai's official count, built around six Leaders, one per colour: Edward.Newgate, Shanks, Rocks.D.Xebec, Kaido, Monkey.D.Luffy and Charlotte Linlin. The set's theme is Elbaph and the Four Emperors. The most expensive card in the set on current C3 data is the Monkey.D.Luffy (079) Super Leader Alternate Art at AU$3,625.00, an outlier well clear of everything else. The most competitively discussed Leader is Rocks.D.Xebec, revealed last on 3 August 2026. English booster boxes are being advertised in Australia anywhere from AU$219.99 to AU$749.99, which is the single most important fact for anyone buying this week.

### The card-count numbers do not agree, and here is why

You will see three different totals quoted for OP-17, so rather than pick one silently, here is what each is counting.

- **131+4 types.** This is Bandai's official figure on the OP-17 product page. It is a count of distinct card *types*, with the +4 sitting outside the main numbered run.
- **126+1.** This appears in some early preview coverage published before the full list was confirmed. It predates the final composition and should be treated as superseded.
- **183 entries.** This is what the [C3 One Piece Card Vault](/cards/onepiece) tracks for the set. It is higher because it counts every separately priced printing as its own entry, so a card and its alternate art are two rows, and it also includes sealed products and promotional cards attached to the set.

None of these is wrong. They answer different questions. If you are building a binder checklist, use Bandai's official type count. If you are pricing a collection, the printing-level count is the one that matters, because the alternate art is where nearly all the money sits.

### What the DON!! cards and the +4 are doing

Two parts of the set structure confuse people, so they are worth separating out.

The **+4** in Bandai's 131+4 figure sits outside the main numbered run. In practice these are the cards that come attached to the product rather than pulled from the standard rarity slots. The **DON!! cards** are the other category the C3 Vault tracks separately, at eight printings for this set, topping out at AU$111.59. DON!! cards are functionally identical whichever version you use, so a premium DON!! is purely a presentation purchase. If someone quotes you a three figure price for a DON!!, that is a collector item and it does nothing for your deck.

This is also why the printing-level count matters more than the type count if you are valuing a collection. A set with 131 types can easily carry 183 separately priced printings, and the difference between those two numbers is where essentially all of the money in OP-17 lives.

## Archetypes and what each colour is doing

The set is themed on Elbaph and the Yonko, and the Leader lineup reflects that directly: three of the six are current or former Emperors, and a fourth, Rocks.D.Xebec, is the man who assembled the crew that produced them.

- **Blue Rocks.D.Xebec** is the archetype absorbing the most competitive attention. Early builds lean on drawing aggressively, converting awkward cards into defensive resources, and rebuilding a board late with the Secret Rare [Rocks.D.Xebec (118)](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-118-op17-118).
- **Black Monkey.D.Luffy** is the Elbaph-outfit Straw Hat Leader, the one Bandai foregrounded in its own product copy.
- **Red Edward.Newgate** pairs with the set's only Treasure Rare, also a Whitebeard card, which is unlikely to be a coincidence in an anniversary set.
- **Purple Kaido**, **Green Shanks** and **Yellow Charlotte Linlin** round out the Emperor theme.

We are deliberately not ranking these Leaders yet. One week of pre-release and simulator play is not a metagame. For an honest read on what the early data does and does not support, see our [OP17 early meta analysis on Rocks.D.Xebec](/blog/op17-early-meta-rocks-d-xebec/).

That is a 3.4 times spread between the cheapest and dearest Australian listing for one product, and the cheapest two are both sold out. Advertised prices are asking prices. They are not evidence of what anyone paid. We have written up what is driving that gap separately in [why OP17 is so expensive in Australia](/blog/why-op17-so-expensive-australia/).

If you are buying singles rather than sealed, compare against live Australian dollar pricing on the [C3 One Piece Card Vault](/cards/onepiece) first, and use [C3 Market Insights](/market) to see where the set sits against the rest of the game before committing.

## How to decide what to buy

- **You want to play a Leader competitively.** Buy the base print. The AU$1.01 Luffy and the AU$1.03 Kaido play identically to the AU$200 versions.
- **You want the chase.** The Treasure Rare Whitebeard and the alternate art Leaders are the defensible collector targets. The Super Leader Luffy is a different category of purchase and carries genuine liquidity risk at this price.
- **You are considering a sealed box.** Wait. Two of the three Australian listings above are already sold out and the third is quoting September at AU$749.99. Buying into a supply squeeze in release week is the most reliable way to overpay in this hobby.
- **You already play One Piece.** The useful question is which OP-17 singles upgrade the deck you own, not whether to switch Leaders.

## What we are not claiming

We have not opened OP-17 product, and nothing here is a review. Every price is a market snapshot dated 26 August 2026, taken days after release, on a set where a third of the Super Rares have no price recorded yet. Card prices this early are noisy, thin on listings, and routinely move by large percentages in the first fortnight. Treat this guide as an accurate map of the set rather than a settled valuation.
