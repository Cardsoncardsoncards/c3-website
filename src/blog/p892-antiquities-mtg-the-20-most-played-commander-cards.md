Antiquities landed back in March of 1994, a small expansion built almost entirely around artifacts, and it has since become one of the more curious sets to track through a Commander lens. Despite its age and its modest card count, several Antiquities cards still see genuine play in EDH decks today, and this list ranks the twenty most played according to EDHREC rank, from the most played card down to the twentieth.

A lower EDHREC rank means a card shows up in more decklists, so Ashnod's Altar sitting at the top with a rank of 132 tells you it is played far more widely than anything else from this set. For cards with more than one printing inside Antiquities itself, we take the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across those printings to decide placement, and we price the entry using whichever printing carries the highest price. Where two printings land on exactly the same price, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved one way or the other.

Most entries on this list are single-printing cards within the set, but a handful, like Strip Mine, Urza's Tower, Urza's Mine, Urza's Power Plant and Mishra's Factory, have four priced printings each, and the spread between the cheapest and the most expensive of those varies a lot from card to card. All prices below are quoted in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Card prices shift over time with the market, so treat any figure here as accurate for the moment it was recorded rather than a permanent number.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Antiquities release?**
Antiquities released on 1994-03-04, making it one of the earliest expansions in Magic's history.

**Which card from Antiquities is the most played in Commander?**
Ashnod's Altar is the most played, sitting at EDHREC rank 132, well ahead of every other card from the set.

**How does this list handle cards with multiple printings inside Antiquities?**
A card is ranked using the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across its printings in the set, and priced using whichever of those printings has the highest price.

**What happens when two printings of the same card share an identical price?**
That tie is noted directly rather than being broken arbitrarily, so readers can see the two printings sit level with each other.
