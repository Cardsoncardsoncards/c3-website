Pokemon 30th Celebration releases globally on 16 September 2026 with one of the widest product lineups Pokemon has put behind a single expansion, spread across five release waves through to December. The prices currently range from AU$125.86 to AU$770.12 for single units, and the differences between them are not always what the packaging suggests. This guide goes product by product and tells you who each one is genuinely for.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** Every figure below is a pre-release asking price, not a completed sale, on a product that has not released. Pre-release pricing on anniversary products moves substantially at release, and this guide will be refreshed within 24 hours of Australian release pricing landing. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

For most people the [Booster Bundle at AU$130.31](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-booster-bundle) is the correct purchase. The set's defining feature, one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations in every pack, is delivered by packs, and no boxed product improves your rate at it. The [Elite Trainer Box at AU$239.19](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-elite-trainer-box) is the right call if you want accessories as well as packs. The [Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box at AU$716.18](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-pokemon-center-elite-trainer-box) is a three times premium over the standard version for broadly comparable card contents, and it is a packaging and scarcity purchase rather than a value one.

## Understanding what you are actually buying

Before the product table, three facts about this set that change the buying logic.

1. **Every card in the set is foil.** There is no filler tier, so the difference between products is not card quality, it is card quantity and packaging.
2. **The Pikachu is pack-linked.** Every booster pack contains five foil cards, one of which is one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations, plus a foil Basic Energy. Your progress toward the thirty depends only on how many packs you open, not on which box they came in.
3. **The chase is very narrow.** The new Futuristic Rare treatment appears on exactly two cards, [Mewtwo ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mewtwo-ex-157-128) and [Mew ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mew-ex-158-128), in a set numbered to 128. No product meaningfully improves your odds at a two card target.

Together those three facts mean the honest comparison between products is close to a price-per-pack calculation plus whatever you value the packaging at.

## The comparison that matters most

The gap people keep asking about is the Elite Trainer Box against the Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box.

| | Standard ETB | Pokemon Center ETB |
|---|---|---|
| Price | AU$239.19 | AU$716.18 |
| Premium | Baseline | 3.0 times |

The card contents of the two are broadly comparable. The premium is for exclusive packaging and constrained distribution through a single channel. That is a legitimate thing to want, and Pokemon Center exclusives do have a history of holding value better than general release products. It is not, however, a value purchase in terms of cards received, and it should not be presented as one.

Our read: if you are opening, buy the standard box or the bundle. If you are sealing away a single showpiece and you accept you are paying for scarcity rather than contents, the Pokemon Center version is the defensible choice. We go deeper on this in [ETB vs Pokemon Center ETB vs Booster Bundle](/blog/pokemon-30th-etb-vs-pokemon-center-etb-vs-booster-bundle/).

## What to buy, by buyer type

**You want to open packs and enjoy the set.** [Booster Bundle, AU$130.31](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-booster-bundle). Lowest entry to the actual product, no packaging premium, full access to the Pikachu mechanic. Worth checking [current 30th Celebration Booster Bundle listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=pokemon+30th+celebration+booster+bundle&campid=5339146789&customid=pokemon-30th-bundle&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) against that tracked figure, since this is the product most likely to be available at close to retail.

**You want to collect all 30 Pikachu.** Packs, in whatever form is cheapest per pack. Nothing else helps. Track progress against our [30 Pikachu checklist](/blog/pokemon-30th-celebration-30-pikachu-cards/).

**You want the accessories too.** [Elite Trainer Box, AU$239.19](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-elite-trainer-box). Sleeves, dice and a storage box alongside the packs.

**You are buying a gift.** The [Figure Collections](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-figure-collection-mew) at AU$183.29 and AU$142.22 present far better than a pack stack, and a 30th anniversary figure has display value independent of card prices.

**You collect Eeveelutions.** The [Sylveon ex tin at AU$140.20](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-ex-tin-sylveon-ex) is the accessible entry. The Ultra-Premium Collections are the Espeon and Umbreon products, and both are worth waiting on until contents are fully confirmed. [Espeon ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-espeon-ex-070-128), [Umbreon ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-umbreon-ex-092-128) and [Sylveon ex](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-sylveon-ex-071-128) are all in the main set regardless.

**You are sealing product away long term.** Pokemon Center ETB or an Ultra-Premium Collection, with the caveat that you are buying an unconfirmed scarcity assumption.

**You play competitively.** Nothing here, yet. This set is not legal for the [2026 World Championships](/blog/pokemon-worlds-2026-tcg-preview/), which conclude before it releases, and its competitive relevance will not be clear for weeks after launch.

## What to avoid overpaying for

**The Classic Collection Pack at AU$361.04.** Thirty reprinted classic cards, of which exactly two are officially confirmed: Base Set Charizard and the Pikachu and Zekrom Tag Team card. You are paying AU$361 to learn what the other 28 are. Wait for the announcement. If the Base Set Charizard reprint is the actual draw for you, buying that single card after release will almost certainly cost less than the pack, and [current 30th Celebration Charizard listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=pokemon+30th+celebration+charizard&campid=5339146789&customid=pokemon-30th-charizard&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) will show whether that holds once the set is out.

**The Ultra-Premium Collections at AU$588.77 and AU$770.12.** Full contents are not confirmed, and there are two versions with a AU$181 gap between them that nobody can currently justify from published information.

**Anything bought on the assumption of no reprints.** No statement has been made about print runs or reprints. The five wave rollout through to 4 December 2026 is not the shape of a constrained release, and much of the current pricing assumes otherwise. We set that argument out in full in [why 30th Celebration is so hyped](/blog/why-pokemon-30th-celebration-is-hyped/).

**Cases.** Unless you are reselling, the case-quantity products above are several thousand dollars of exposure to a single set.

## Sealed collector versus opener: the actual decision

This is the question underneath every product choice above, and it is worth answering directly rather than leaving implied.

**If you are opening**, the only variable that matters is cost per pack. Packaging, exclusive art on a box and bonus accessories do not change what comes out of a booster. Because every card in this set is foil and the Pikachu is guaranteed per pack, the pack itself is unusually consistent in what it delivers, which makes the cost-per-pack comparison cleaner than in a normal set. On current pricing the Booster Bundle is the clear answer, with the standard Elite Trainer Box second if you want the accessories thrown in.

**If you are sealing**, cost per pack is close to irrelevant and the only variable that matters is how constrained the product will be in two years. That is unknowable today, but the observable proxies are distribution channel and production scale. A product sold exclusively through one channel, such as the Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box, has a structurally better scarcity argument than a general release product, regardless of what is inside it.

**The trap is buying a sealing product and opening it, or an opening product and sealing it.** A sealed Booster Bundle is unlikely to appreciate meaningfully because there will be a great many of them. An opened Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box has destroyed the exact thing you paid the three times premium for.

Decide which you are before you buy, not after the parcel arrives.

## Value per pack, and its limits

We can compare the pack-bearing products on price, but we want to be careful not to present a precise expected-value calculation this set does not support.

What we can say: the Booster Bundle at AU$130.31 is the cheapest route to packs in the lineup, and the Mini Tin Display at AU$408.07 is the bulk-opening option for anyone wanting volume in a single purchase.

What we cannot say: what a pack is worth in expectation. That would require confirmed pull rates for the Futuristic Rares, Special Illustration Rares and Illustration Rares, and none have been published. Any article giving you an expected value per pack for this set before release is estimating, not calculating. We will revisit once real pull data exists, in the same way we track it for other sets through the [C3 EV calculator](/ev-calculator).

We tracked The Hobbit's twenty most expensive cards from preorder to three weeks post-release. Every one fell. The basket lost 73.1 per cent. The best performer still lost 38.1 per cent. The [full comparison is here](/blog/the-hobbit-preorder-vs-post-release-prices/).

## Australian availability notes

The simultaneous 16 September global release removes the historical disadvantage where Australian stock arrived after overseas markets had already set prices. Everyone gets it at once.

The products most likely to be allocation constrained here are the Pokemon Center exclusives and the Ultra-Premium Collections, which is already visible in their pricing. General release products, the Booster Bundle and standard Elite Trainer Box, are the least likely to be squeezed.
