Legends arrived on 1 June 1994, one of the earliest expansions Magic ever produced, and it remains a set collectors and Commander players return to for its odd mix of format staples and curiosities. This list ranks the twenty most played Legends cards in Commander, using EDHREC rank as the measure, where a lower number means the card sees more play across decks tracked by EDHREC. The order runs from number 1 at the top, the most played card in the set, down to number 20.

Legends had a habit of printing a card once and never touching it again within the set, and that holds for every entry here: each of these twenty cards has a single Legends printing. Where a set does carry more than one printing of a card, our methodology takes the lowest EDHREC rank as the card's position and the highest priced printing as its headline figure, and if two printings land on the same price to the cent, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved. None of that machinery comes into play for this particular list, since every card here is a singleton, but it's worth stating the rule plainly.

Prices are quoted in Australian dollars and reflect a snapshot in time. Singles markets move, sometimes quickly, so treat every figure below as accurate to when it was recorded rather than a permanent number.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did the Legends set release?**
Legends released on 1 June 1994, making it one of the older sets tracked in this kind of Commander play data.

**Which Legends card is the most played in Commander?**
Mana Drain is the most played, holding an EDHREC rank of 117 and priced at AU$356.94.

**How does the ranking handle cards with more than one printing in the set?**
A card's position is set by its lowest, meaning best, EDHREC rank across its printings, and its listed price is the highest priced printing among them. If two printings share an identical price to the cent, that tie is noted explicitly rather than resolved silently, though none of the twenty cards on this list required that step.

**Where can Australian buyers purchase these singles?**
Singles are available through the card pages on Cards on Cards on Cards, as well as through eBay Australia.

**Are these prices fixed?**
No. Every price shown is a snapshot in Australian dollars and can move up or down over time as the singles market shifts.

**Why does a low priced card sometimes rank higher than an expensive one?**
EDHREC rank measures how often a card appears in tracked Commander decks, which is independent of price. The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale, for example, carries the highest price on this list at AU$4117.99 but ranks lower in play frequency than much cheaper cards like Mana Drain or Sylvan Library.

**Does rarity in Legends correlate with placement on this list?**
Not consistently. The list mixes commons, uncommons and rares throughout, with commons like Chain Lightning and Holy Day appearing well above some rares, and rares like The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale sitting near the bottom despite carrying the highest price.
