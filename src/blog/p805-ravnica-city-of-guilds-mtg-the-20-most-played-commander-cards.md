Ravnica: City of Guilds arrived on 2005-10-07 as the set that introduced the ten guilds to Magic: The Gathering, and it left behind a strange legacy in Commander. Cards designed for a block about faction identity turned out to include some of the most enduring mana fixing and ramp tools the format has ever used, and that shows up clearly once you rank the set by how often its cards appear in decks today.

This list orders the twenty most played cards from the set using EDHREC rank, where a lower number means the card shows up in more decks. Farseek sits at the top with a rank of 23, and the list runs down to Hour of Reckoning at rank 1043. Every one of these twenty cards has just a single priced printing in the set, so there are no ties to note and no split between a headline price and a secondary printing this time round, though that arithmetic still matters for reading the numbers correctly.

Prices are listed in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Singles can be bought through our own card pages or through eBay Australia, and both markets shift over time, so treat every figure here as a point in time rather than a fixed value.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Ravnica: City of Guilds release?**
The set released on 2005-10-07.

**Which card is the most played from this set in Commander?**
Farseek is the most played card, sitting at EDHREC rank 23, the lowest rank number of any card from the set.

**How is this list ranked and priced?**
Cards are ranked by EDHREC rank, from lowest number to highest, with lower numbers meaning more decks. When a card has multiple printings in the set, the ranking uses its best rank and the price shown is its highest priced printing, with any exact price ties noted rather than broken silently. In this particular list every card has just one priced printing, so no ties or splits apply.

**Where can Australian readers buy these singles?**
Singles can be bought through the card pages on our own site as well as through eBay Australia.

**Do the prices in this list stay accurate over time?**
No, every price listed is a snapshot taken at one point in time, and prices for these cards move afterwards as the market shifts.

**Are all twenty cards from the same rarity tier?**
No, the list mixes rarities, including several rares, a range of commons, and one uncommon, Putrefy.

**Why does a card like Doubling Season have such a high price but a middling rank?**
Rank and price measure different things, one reflects how many decks include the card and the other reflects market value, so a card can be expensive without being among the very highest ranked.
