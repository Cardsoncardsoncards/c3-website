Born of the Gods is the second set in the Theros block, released on 2014-02-07. It brought the gods to the forefront of Standard at the time, but its long tail in Commander is what this list looks at. Rather than tracking tournament results or set value, we have pulled the twenty cards from Born of the Gods with the strongest EDHREC rank, which is a proxy for how often each card turns up in published Commander decklists.

The list runs from number 1, the most played card in the set according to EDHREC, down to number 20. A lower rank number means a card appears in more decks, so Temple of Enlightenment sitting at rank 314 puts it well ahead of everything else here. Where a card has more than one printing within Born of the Gods, we take its best available rank for placement and its highest priced printing for the headline figure. Any case where two printings land on exactly the same price is called out rather than quietly resolved, though as it happens every card on this particular list has just one priced printing in the set.

Every price below is a snapshot in Australian dollars. Singles markets move week to week, sometimes day to day, so treat these figures as a point in time rather than a fixed number. You can find current listings for any of these cards through the individual card pages on our own site, or by checking eBay Australia for additional supply.

What follows is the full rundown, one entry per card, with commentary on how each sits relative to its neighbours on the list.

**When was Born of the Gods released?**

**What does this list actually measure?**
It ranks the twenty Born of the Gods cards with the strongest EDHREC rank, which reflects how frequently each card appears in published Commander decklists rather than its tournament performance or set value.

**Which card is the most played from this set?**

**How are cards with multiple printings in the set handled?**
A card is placed using its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across its printings, and priced using its highest priced printing. Where two printings share the exact same price, that tie is noted rather than resolved silently.
