The Barcelona Regional Qualifier ran from 21 to 23 August 2026 and drew more than 2,130 players, making it the largest test the Vendetta metagame has faced. It produced the cleanest possible illustration of a distinction we made five days before it happened: the most popular deck and the winning deck are different questions. Kennen was brought by 271 players, converted better than anything else and posted the best win rate in the field. Ornn, which almost nobody was talking about, beat it in the semi-final and again in the final.

**Data checked: 1 September 2026.** Standings, records, legend representation and conversion commentary below are from the published tournament record for the Barcelona Regional Qualifier. Card prices are live C3 Card Vault figures in Australian dollars where a live price exists. Several Riftbound legend cards currently carry no price in the Vault, and those are linked without a figure rather than estimated.

## Quick answer

**TheManLand won the Barcelona Regional Qualifier playing Ornn**, finishing 14-1-1, after defeating Kennen in both the semi-final and the final. Runner-up was **Koko Lopez on Kennen, Heart of the Tempest** at 13-1-2. **Kennen was the format's dominant legend by every measure except the trophy**: most represented with 271 players, best conversion rate, best win rate, and the only legend to put three players into the Top 8.

## Kennen dominated everything except the final

It is worth being precise about how strong Kennen's weekend actually was, because the trophy obscures it.

**Most brought.** 271 players registered Kennen, Heart of the Tempest, more than any other legend.

**Best conversion rate.** Of the legends with meaningful representation, Kennen converted Day 1 entries into the cut at the highest rate. That is the single most useful metric in a large Swiss event, because it controls for popularity in a way that raw Top 8 counts do not.

**Best win rate.** Kennen also posted the highest win rate in the field.

**Three of eight.** No other legend managed more than two.

A deck that is the most played, the best converting, the highest win rate and the most represented in the cut is the best deck in the format on any ordinary reading. It simply did not win the tournament, because a single-elimination bracket is a small sample and Ornn was built to beat it.

## Ornn was a counter-pick, and it worked exactly once

The relevant fact about TheManLand's Ornn is that it was a deliberate answer to Kennen rather than a general-purpose good deck. It met Kennen in the semi-final and the final and beat it both times.

That is a meaningfully different claim from "Ornn is the best deck". A counter-pick is a bet on what your opponents will bring, and in a field where 271 people brought the same legend it was an unusually well-priced bet. Whether Ornn holds up once players expect it is the open question, and the answer will come from the next major event rather than from this one.

**Ornn is also not a Vendetta legend.** [Ornn, Fire Below the Mountain](/cards/riftbound/spiritforged-ornn-fire-below-the-mountain-189-221) comes from Spiritforged, as does its support piece [Ornn's Forge](/cards/riftbound/spiritforged-ornns-forge-213-221) at AU$0.22. The card that won the biggest Vendetta-era event is not from Vendetta, which is a pattern this format keeps producing.

## What our earlier read got right, and what it missed

We published a Vendetta metagame analysis on 26 August 2026, five days before this event, based on sixteen tournaments of 64 or more players. Checking it against Barcelona:

**Right: Kennen leads the format.** We had Kennen, Heart of the Tempest at roughly 9 per cent metashare and called it "the standout Vendetta legend and the clearest case of the new set genuinely adding a top-tier option". Barcelona confirmed that emphatically, and by a wider margin than 9 per cent suggested.

**Right: Master Yi is a genuine contender.** We listed Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman as following Kennen. It took two Top 8 slots.

**Right, and this is the one that matters: we warned that metashare is not results.** The exact wording was "metashare measures what people brought, not what won. A 9 per cent metashare tells you Kennen was the most popular choice, not that it was the best one." Barcelona is the cleanest possible demonstration. Kennen was the most brought, the best converting and the highest win rate, and it still lost the final to a deck designed to beat it.

**Missed: Ornn.** We identified only four Vendetta legends with recorded Top 8 finishes, being Kennen, Nasus, Mel and Ambessa, and Ornn was not on our radar at all. In fairness it is a Spiritforged legend rather than a Vendetta one, so it fell outside the frame of a Vendetta-focused analysis, but that is an explanation rather than an excuse. A metagame article that only tracks new-set legends will miss counter-picks from older sets, which is precisely what decided this event.

**Missed: Azir and Rengar.** Neither appeared in our read, and both made the cut.

## What this means for Australian players

**The format is cheap to enter and expensive to collect.** Nothing in the Top 8 requires a significant outlay in playable printings. If you want to test Kennen or Ornn, the barrier is close to nothing.

**Expect Ornn interest to rise, and expect it to be short-lived.** Counter-picks get copied after they win and then get answered. Buying into Ornn now on the strength of one bracket is speculation on a deck whose entire premise was surprise.

**Kennen remains the deck to beat.** If you are preparing for a local event, prepare for Kennen. It was the most represented legend by a distance and the strongest by every rate-based measure, and one loss in a bracket does not undo that.

## Why conversion rate is the number to read

One habit worth building from this event, because Riftbound coverage rarely does it well.

**Top 8 counts are popularity in disguise.** If 271 players bring Kennen and 40 bring Ornn, Kennen will usually place more players in the cut even if the two decks are equally good, simply because there are more of them. Counting Top 8 slots and calling the winner the best deck is circular.

**Conversion rate controls for that.** It asks what proportion of the players who brought a legend made the cut, which is the closest thing a Swiss event gives you to a like-for-like comparison. Kennen had the best conversion rate in a field of more than 2,130 players, and that is a far stronger claim than three Top 8 slots on its own.

**Win rate is the third leg.** Kennen also led on win rate. A legend that is simultaneously the most brought, the best converting and the highest winning is not carried by popularity, because popularity would drag its rates down toward the average, not above it.

So the correct reading of Barcelona is that Kennen is the best deck in the format and lost one bracket to a targeted answer. Anyone writing that Ornn "dethroned" Kennen on the strength of two matches is reading the trophy and ignoring the dataset underneath it.

## The next meta test

The Singapore Regional Qualifier runs from 4 to 6 September 2026 at Singapore EXPO with a US$25,000 prize pool, and it is the penultimate Regional Qualifier of the year before Los Angeles from 25 to 27 September. Singapore is the event that answers the two questions Barcelona left open: whether Ornn survives being expected, and whether Kennen's rate dominance converts into a trophy when the bracket falls differently.
