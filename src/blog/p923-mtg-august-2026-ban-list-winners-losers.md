On 10 August 2026 Wizards of the Coast banned three cards in Standard, banned one in Legacy and restricted the same card in Vintage. The timing was unusual: four days before The Hobbit released and four days before Standard Regional Championship Qualifiers opened. Two weeks on there is enough post-ban tournament data and enough price movement to say what actually changed.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** Ban details are from the official Wizards announcement of 10 August 2026. Metagame data is from Wizards' own Metagame Mentor analysis of nearly 300 successful tournament decks from 11 to 17 August 2026. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

Standard lost [Badgermole Cub](/cards/mtg/badgermole-cub), [Stormchaser's Talent](/cards/mtg/stormchasers-talent) and [Gran-Gran](/cards/mtg/gran-gran). Legacy lost [The Fantasticar](/cards/mtg/the-fantasticar), which was simultaneously restricted in Vintage. Pioneer, Modern, Pauper, Alchemy, Historic, Timeless and both Brawl formats were untouched. The stated trigger was Selesnya Offense posting over a 57 per cent win rate while holding the second-highest play rate. The format opened dramatically: three decks held over half the winner's metagame before the bans, and afterwards sixteen archetypes shared it with none above 13 per cent.

## What was banned, format by format

### Standard, banned

| Card | Current AUD | Wizards' stated reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| [Badgermole Cub](/cards/mtg/badgermole-cub) | AU$89.52 | Demanded immediate answers, created an insurmountable mana advantage |
| [Stormchaser's Talent](/cards/mtg/stormchasers-talent) | AU$5.32 | Inappropriately threatening for a one mana card |
| [Gran-Gran](/cards/mtg/gran-gran) | AU$0.41 | Excessive late game value for its cost |

The framing Wizards used is worth quoting because it explains the whole announcement: decks that maintain high win rates while also having high play rates are large threats to the balance of formats. Selesnya Offense was the specific trigger, with over a 57 per cent win rate on Arena alongside the second-highest play rate in the format.

Note that the three banned cards came from three different decks, not one. The three most prominent decks before the announcement were Izzet Prowess, Selesnya Offense and Jeskai Lessons, which together made up over half the winner's metagame. Each lost a key card.

### Legacy, banned

[The Fantasticar](/cards/mtg/the-fantasticar), currently AU$11.45. Wizards described it as appearing in over a dozen different archetypes, making it near impossible to address with any single series of cards. It enabled both all-in combo strategies like Doomsday and fairer shells, creating an unavoidable metagame compression.

### Vintage, restricted

The Fantasticar, restricted to a single copy rather than banned outright. The stated concern was its effect in Glaring Fleshraker-based Shops decks, with restriction intended to limit its impact while allowing it to persist as a one-of.

### Untouched

**Pioneer:** no changes, described as healthy with plenty of viable decks. Izzet Spells and Badgermole Cub variants held acceptable rates.

**Modern:** no changes, despite Eldrazi Trudge posting a 65 per cent win rate at Pro Tour Amsterdam. Wizards considered this typical of Modern's polar matchups and observed organic self-correction on Magic Online.

That Modern decision is the most interesting non-action in the announcement. A 65 per cent win rate at a Pro Tour would trigger a ban in most formats, and Wizards explicitly chose to treat Modern's higher variance as normal rather than as a problem.

**Pauper, Alchemy, Historic, Timeless, Brawl, Competitive Brawl:** no changes.

## The winners

The clearest winner is the format itself, and the numbers show it plainly.

Before the bans, three decks held over half the winner's metagame. After, across nearly 300 successful tournament decks from 11 to 17 August:

| Rank | Archetype | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mono-Green Landfall | 13.0% |
| 2 | Dimir Midrange | 10.6% |
| 3 | Mardu Discard | 9.8% |
| 4 | Izzet Spellementals | 8.9% |
| 5 | Orzhov Life Gain | 8.3% |
| 6 | Jeskai Lessons | 6.9% |
| 7 | Dimir Excruciator | 5.9% |
| 8 | Four-Color Control | 5.8% |
| 9 | Mono-Red Aggro | 5.0% |
| 10 | Azorius Prison | 4.3% |
| 11 | Boros Dragons | 3.7% |
| 12 | Sultai Reanimator | 2.0% |

No deck above 13 per cent, sixteen named archetypes and a 9.8 per cent "other" category. That is a substantially healthier distribution than the one that prompted the action.

**Specific winners:**

- **Mono-Green Landfall.** Now the top deck at 13.0 per cent despite losing Badgermole Cub. Wizards' own analysis notes the card was never an essential piece of the deck, which is a good example of a ban hitting a card rather than the deck that made it famous.
- **Dimir Midrange and Mardu Discard.** Neither lost anything, and both moved into the vacuum at 10.6 and 9.8 per cent.
- **Orzhov Life Gain and Azorius Prison.** Slower, grindier strategies that struggle when the format is dominated by efficient aggressive decks. Both are now established at 8.3 and 4.3 per cent.
- **The Hobbit's fringe archetypes.** Boros Dwarves and Five-Color Humans found room they would not have had, using cards like [Thorin, Mountain-king](/cards/mtg/thorin-mountain-king) at AU$39.97, [The Queen of Dale](/cards/mtg/the-queen-of-dale) at AU$44.63, [Kíli the Resourceful](/cards/mtg/k-li-the-resourceful) at AU$3.71 and [Dwarven Mauler](/cards/mtg/dwarven-mauler) at AU$0.28. We cover that in [how The Hobbit is changing Standard](/blog/the-hobbit-standard-meta-smaug-2026/).

## The losers

**Jeskai Lessons** lost Gran-Gran and Wizards note it became more difficult, though the deck survived at 6.9 per cent. Players adapted rather than abandoning it.

**Izzet Prowess** lost Stormchaser's Talent. It does not appear as a named archetype in the post-ban top 12, with Izzet Spellementals appearing instead at 8.9 per cent.

**Selesnya Offense**, the deck that triggered the action, does not appear in the post-ban top 12 at all.

**Anyone holding the banned cards at the wrong moment.** Which brings us to the part most ban coverage gets wrong.

## What actually happened to the prices

This is the section worth reading if you own cards, and the finding is counterintuitive.

[Badgermole Cub](/cards/mtg/badgermole-cub) is still AU$89.52. That is higher than any card in The Hobbit, including [Smaug the Magnificent](/cards/mtg/smaug-the-magnificent) at AU$73.82. A card banned in Standard two weeks ago is more expensive than the most-played new card in Standard.

The reason is format breadth. Badgermole Cub remains legal and played in Pioneer, where Wizards specifically noted Badgermole Cub variants holding acceptable rates, and it has Commander demand. A Standard ban removes one source of demand from a card that has several.

Compare with the other two:

- [Stormchaser's Talent](/cards/mtg/stormchasers-talent), AU$5.32
- [Gran-Gran](/cards/mtg/gran-gran), AU$0.41

Both were far more Standard-dependent, and both now sit at prices reflecting a much narrower set of homes.

**The rule this suggests:** the price impact of a ban is roughly proportional to how much of a card's demand came from the banned format. A card with Commander, Pioneer and Modern homes barely notices. A card that only mattered in Standard falls hard.

[The Fantasticar](/cards/mtg/the-fantasticar) at AU$11.45 is the interesting middle case: banned in Legacy, restricted in Vintage, but it originates from Marvel Super Heroes Commander, so its Commander demand is untouched by either decision.

## Replacement cards and what to buy

The post-ban format is cheap to enter, which is unusual after a large ban.

The top archetypes are established decks that lost nothing, so their cards are already priced by an existing market rather than spiking on new demand. The genuinely new options, the Hobbit-enabled Dwarves and Humans shells, are built almost entirely from cards under AU$5: [Dáin's Company](/cards/mtg/d-ins-company) at AU$2.30, [Dáin Ironfoot](/cards/mtg/d-in-ironfoot) at AU$1.19, [Bofur, Reliable Guardian](/cards/mtg/bofur-reliable-guardian-concerted-care) at AU$0.48, [Thorin Oakenshield](/cards/mtg/thorin-oakenshield) at AU$0.43, [Key to the Side-Door](/cards/mtg/key-to-the-side-door) at AU$0.34 and [Dwarven Mattock](/cards/mtg/dwarven-mattock) at AU$0.24.

Meanwhile the whole Hobbit singles market corrected hard after release, with the top 20 down 73.1 per cent from preorder. The [full comparison is here](/blog/the-hobbit-preorder-vs-post-release-prices/), and the practical upshot is that entering Standard right now is cheaper than it has been in some time.

## What Australian players should do

1. **Check your Standard deck against the three bans.** If you played Izzet Prowess, Selesnya Offense or Jeskai Lessons, you are affected.
2. **Do not sell banned cards reflexively.** The Badgermole Cub example shows why. Ask where else the card is played before deciding.
3. **RCQ season is live.** The bans landed four days before Standard RCQs opened, so the format you qualify in is the post-ban one.
4. **The entry cost is low right now.** Post-ban plus post-release-correction is an unusually good moment to build.

## What happens next

The next Banned and Restricted announcement date has not been reached, and Wizards discussed these changes on WeeklyMTG on 11 August 2026.

The things worth watching: whether Mono-Green Landfall's 13.0 per cent hardens into dominance, whether Modern's Eldrazi Trudge self-corrects as Wizards expect, and whether the RCQ season produces a large enough physical dataset to confirm or contradict the one week Magic Online sample this analysis rests on.
