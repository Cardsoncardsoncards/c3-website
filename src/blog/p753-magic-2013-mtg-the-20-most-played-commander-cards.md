Magic 2013, the last of the core sets to arrive as an annual boxed release before Wizards shook up the format, hit shelves on the 13th of July 2012. It's a set built around fixing and value, all of which explains why so many of its cards still show up in Commander decks a decade on. This list ranks the 20 most played Magic 2013 cards in the format, using EDHREC rank, where a lower number means more decks are running that card.

Each card on this list appears once, even if it had several printings within Magic 2013 itself. Where a card carries multiple printings in the set, its position is set by the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank of those printings, and its listed price is the highest of them. If two printings land on exactly the same price, that tie is noted rather than resolved by any hidden tiebreaker. As it happens, every card here has just the one printing in this set, so no tie situations arise this time.

Sitting at the top of the list is Reliquary Tower, the single most played Magic 2013 card in Commander decks by EDHREC's measure. From there the list runs down through a mix of the set's dual lands, ramp pieces, and a scattering of rares and mythics that found long-term homes in the format.

Prices throughout are quoted in Australian dollars and reflect a snapshot only. Singles markets move, sometimes by a lot, so treat every figure here as a point in time rather than a fixed number. Readers in Australia can pick these cards up through our own card pages or through eBay Australia, and it's worth checking both before buying.

Magic 2013 released on the 13th of July 2012, and it has been available for singles purchase ever since.

**Which card is the most played Magic 2013 card in Commander?**

**How was this list put together?**
Each card appears once, ranked by EDHREC rank, from most played at number 1 down to least played at number 20 among these twenty cards.

**How are cards with multiple printings in the set handled?**
A card with several printings in Magic 2013 is ranked by its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank, and priced by whichever of its printings costs the most. If two printings share a price exactly, that tie is noted rather than being silently broken.
