Rivals of Ixalan released on 19 January 2018, the second and smaller half of the Ixalan block, and it still turns up in Commander decks today thanks to a handful of cards that found permanent homes in dinosaur, pirate and vampire strategies. This list ranks the twenty most played cards from the set in Commander, using EDHREC rank as the measure, where a lower number means the card sees more play across decks tracked by that site. Number one is the most played card in the set, and the list counts down from there to twentieth.

Every card here has only a single priced printing within Rivals of Ixalan itself, so the methodology note about multiple printings and tied prices is mostly a formality for this particular set. Where a set does carry several printings of the same card, our approach is to rank by the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank the card achieves, and to price it by whichever printing costs the most. Any exact price tie between printings gets called out rather than quietly resolved.

Prices below are quoted in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Singles markets move constantly, so treat every figure as accurate at time of writing rather than a permanent number. You can buy any of these cards through the card pages on our own site or through eBay Australia, and it is worth checking both before settling on a source.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Rivals of Ixalan release?**
Rivals of Ixalan released on 19 January 2018 as the second set in the Ixalan block.

**What does this list measure, and how are ties or multiple printings handled?**
The list ranks the twenty most played cards from the set in Commander using EDHREC rank, where a lower number means more play. Cards with several printings in the set would be ranked by their best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank and priced by their highest priced printing, with any exact price tie between printings noted rather than resolved, though every card on this particular list has only one priced printing.

**Which card from Rivals of Ixalan is the most played in Commander?**
Evolving Wilds is the most played card from the set, sitting at EDHREC rank 19, well ahead of every other card on this list.

**Are the prices listed here fixed?**
No, every price is a snapshot in Australian dollars taken at time of writing. Singles prices move over time, so figures should be treated as a point-in-time reference rather than a permanent value.

**Does a higher price mean a card is more played?**
Not necessarily. Several cards on this list, such as Ravenous Chupacabra, rank higher in play than costlier cards further up the list, showing that price and EDHREC rank do not always move together.

**Why do some cards on the list share rarities but very different ranks?**
Rank reflects how often a card appears in tracked Commander decks, not its rarity or print run. Commons like Evolving Wilds and Negate can outrank mythics simply because of how widely they are played across many different deck strategies.
