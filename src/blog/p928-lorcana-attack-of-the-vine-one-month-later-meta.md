Attack of the Vine released on 17 July 2026, which gives roughly six weeks of Core Constructed results to work from rather than release-week impressions. The headline is that the format is genuinely healthy, with no deck above 18 per cent, and that the set's most visible effect has been on collector pricing rather than on the competitive hierarchy.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The window covered is the 17 July release to 26 August 2026, roughly six weeks rather than the round month in the title. Metagame figures describe the Core Constructed format going into the North American Championship. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

Amber Amethyst Midrange leads at 18 per cent, followed by Amber Emerald Elinor and Amber Ruby Boost, with Amber Emerald Songs and Amber Steel Steelsong both at 9 per cent. Amber appears in every top archetype, which is the format's defining structural feature. The strategic spread is wide, running from aggressive lore rushers through evasive questers to slower control shells. On the collector side the set is carrying substantial value, led by [Belle & Beast, Certain as the Sun](/cards/lorcana/attack-of-the-vine-belle-beast-certain-as-the-sun-iconic-245-207) at AU$3,137.96.

**No single deck owns the format.** An 18 per cent leader with a 9 per cent second tier is a healthy distribution by any standard. For context, [Star Wars Unlimited suspended a leader](/blog/star-wars-unlimited-cad-bane-suspension-explained/) that passed 20 per cent, and [MTG banned three cards](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/) in a format where three decks held over half the winner's metagame. Lorcana is in a better competitive state than either.

**The strategic spread is real.** The top of the table contains aggressive lore rushers, evasive questers that go under the opponent, and slower control shells built to answer both. Those are three genuinely different ways to win, not three variations on one plan. Formats where the top decks disagree about how to win are the healthiest kind.

## The Amber question

Here is the finding worth dwelling on, because nobody seems to be saying it plainly.

**Every named top archetype contains Amber.** Amber Amethyst, Amber Emerald, Amber Ruby, Amber Steel. Four of Lorcana's six inks appear as Amber's partner, and Amber itself never sits out.

There are two readings and one weekend of results will separate them.

**The benign reading:** Amber is a flexible support ink that pairs well with everything, and the format's genuine diversity lives in the second ink and the strategy, which is where the aggressive, evasive and control split actually sits. Under this reading Amber is glue, not a problem.

**The concerning reading:** Amber is simply the best ink, and its universality is the format quietly telling you so. Under this reading the diversity is superficial, because every deck is making the same first decision.

We do not have the data to settle this, and we would rather set out both readings than pick one for effect. The [North American Championship](/blog/lorcana-north-american-championship-2026-preview/) on 28 to 30 August is the first good test: if a non-Amber deck wins a 136 player invite-only field, that is a meaningful answer.

## What Attack of the Vine actually changed

This is where a one month retrospective earns its place, and the honest assessment is mixed.

**The set did not reset the format.** Six weeks on, the archetype names are ink pairings and strategies rather than "the new Attack of the Vine deck". That is a set that added tools to an existing framework rather than replacing it.

That is not a criticism, and it is the normal outcome. We have now watched the same pattern in three games this month. [Riftbound Vendetta](/blog/riftbound-vendetta-most-played-cards/) added two viable legends out of four and left Irelia's staple list essentially unchanged. [One Piece OP-17](/blog/op17-early-meta-rocks-d-xebec/) has no physical results yet but the OP16 decks have not gone anywhere. [The Hobbit](/blog/the-hobbit-standard-meta-smaug-2026/) contributed the most-played new card in Standard while the top three decks remained non-Hobbit decks.

**The pattern across all four:** a new set adds options, strengthens fringe strategies, and leaves the top of the format broadly intact. Release-week coverage almost always predicts otherwise, and is almost always wrong.

## Why the Enchanted and Iconic tier holds up better than most premium tiers

Worth examining, because Attack of the Vine's premium pricing has held at levels that several comparable sets in other games have not managed.

**The cards are not gated behind competitive relevance.** In a game where premium treatments land on tournament staples, the price rises and falls with the metagame. A card that gets displaced loses its collector floor along with its competitive one. Lorcana's Enchanted and Iconic cards are wanted for the artwork and the character pairing, which do not rotate.

**The duo theme is a completion mechanic in disguise.** Once a set establishes that its premium tier is famous Disney duos, collectors stop chasing individual cards and start chasing the theme. Completion behaviour supports prices far more steadily than speculation does, because it produces sustained buying rather than a spike.

**Disney's character library is the moat.** Belle and Beast, Lilo and Stitch, Woody and Buzz, Mickey and Minnie are recognisable to people who have never played a card game. That audience is larger than Lorcana's player base and is not affected by anything that happens at a championship.

The counter-argument is the one we would apply to any premium tier: these prices reflect what a small number of buyers will pay for scarce printings, and a set that gets reprinted or a franchise that loses momentum would move them. Nothing in the current data suggests either, but "nothing suggests it" is not the same as "it cannot happen".

## Breakout versus disappointment

**The breakout is not a card, it is the ink.** Amber's universality across every top archetype is the most consequential outcome of this format.

**The disappointment is the absence of a signature deck.** Six weeks on there is no archetype that people call the Attack of the Vine deck. For a set with this much collector momentum, that is a gap.

**The genuine success is format health.** No deck above 18 per cent, three distinct strategic approaches at the top, and no ban conversation. Plenty of games would take that.

## The sealed picture

Worth a note, because the sealed side of Attack of the Vine has behaved differently from the singles.

A [Booster Box](/cards/lorcana/attack-of-the-vine-disney-lorcana-attack-of-the-vine-booster-box) at AU$295.96 sits against a premium tier where a single Iconic card runs to AU$3,137.96. That ratio is what drives box-opening interest, and it is also what makes it a poor plan: the reason the top card is worth ten boxes is that almost nobody opens one.

The case-quantity products, [Booster Box Case](/cards/lorcana/attack-of-the-vine-disney-lorcana-attack-of-the-vine-booster-box-case) at AU$1,213.64, [Prerelease Box Case](/cards/lorcana/attack-of-the-vine-disney-lorcana-attack-of-the-vine-prerelease-box-case) at AU$1,117.40 and [Illumineer's Trove Case](/cards/lorcana/attack-of-the-vine-disney-lorcana-attack-of-the-vine-illumineers-trove-case) at AU$557.15, are retailer-scale purchases rather than collector ones.

For most Australian buyers the sensible route to a specific Enchanted card remains buying that card. Six weeks of post-release supply has already brought the singles market to a workable level, which is more than can be said for a set in its first fortnight. ## What Australian players should do

1. **Learn Amber.** Whatever your second ink, the evidence says Amber is where to start.
2. **Do not buy Enchanted or Iconic cards to play with.** The ordinary printings of the same cards cost a small fraction. The table above is a collector market.
3. **Wait for the Championship before committing.** Two days away, and a 136 player invite-only field will tell you more than six weeks of scattered results.
4. **If you collect, the duos are the theme.** That design decision is driving the entire premium tier and is unlikely to reverse.
