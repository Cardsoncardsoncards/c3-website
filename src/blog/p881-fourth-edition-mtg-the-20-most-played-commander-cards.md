Fourth Edition landed back on the 1st of April 1995, a core set reprint that gathered up a huge slab of Magic's early staples into one accessible package. It has no set symbol on the cards themselves, but it holds a strange kind of density: because it reprinted so many format-defining cards in one go, it turns up again and again in Commander decklists three decades later. This list ranks the 20 most played Fourth Edition cards in Commander today, using EDHREC rank, where a lower number means the card sees more play.

Every card here appears only once on the list, even where it has multiple printings within Fourth Edition itself. Where a card has several printings in the set, we take its lowest (best) EDHREC rank to place it, and its highest priced printing to set the headline figure. If two printings land on exactly the same price, that tie is noted rather than quietly resolved. As it happens, every card in this particular twenty has just one priced printing in Fourth Edition, so the arithmetic stays simple this time round.

Prices below are quoted in Australian dollars and reflect a snapshot at time of writing. Singles markets move, sometimes quickly, so treat every figure as a point in time rather than a fixed number. Readers can chase these prices down through the card pages on our own site or through eBay Australia, where Fourth Edition copies circulate regularly given how large the print run was.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Fourth Edition released?**
Fourth Edition released on the 1st of April 1995 as a core set reprint.

**Which card is the most played Fourth Edition card in Commander?**
Swords to Plowshares tops the list, sitting at EDHREC rank 11 and priced at AU$10.25.

**How does the ranking handle cards with multiple printings in the set?**
Each card appears once, using its lowest (best) EDHREC rank across its Fourth Edition printings and the price of its highest priced printing. Where two printings share an identical price, that tie is noted rather than resolved silently, though every card in this twenty had only one priced printing.

**Where can I buy these singles in Australia?**
Readers can find these cards through the card pages on our own site as well as through eBay Australia, where Fourth Edition copies are common given the size of the original print run.

**Are these prices fixed?**
No. Every price quoted here is a snapshot in Australian dollars at time of writing, and singles prices move over time, sometimes significantly.

**What does EDHREC rank actually measure here?**
It reflects how frequently a card is played in Commander decks as tracked by EDHREC, with a lower number meaning more play, not how expensive or rare the card is.

**Why do some cheaper cards rank higher than expensive ones on this list?**
Play rank and price are independent figures. Mana Vault, for example, carries the highest price on the list but ranks lower in play than several much cheaper cards like Llanowar Elves.
