On 24 August 2026, Cad Bane, Still Faster Than You was suspended from the Star Wars Unlimited Premier format. The official announcement is unusually direct about why: the leader was described as a development mistake, one that was not identified as the strongest option in its colour pairing during playtesting. Two days on, this is what actually happened and what it changes.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The suspension took effect two days ago, so there is no meaningful post-suspension tournament data yet. Everything below about the future is labelled as expectation rather than result. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

Cad Bane, Still Faster Than You is suspended from Premier, effective 24 August 2026. The official reasoning is that it made up over 20 per cent of the metagame while also converting to top cut extremely well, and that the design was a mistake rather than a balance drift. A suspension is presented as temporary rather than permanent, with the format to be reviewed, though no return date has been committed to. The next stated checkpoint is the Homeworlds meta check-in, and Homeworlds is due in October 2026.

## What the official announcement says

The key points, drawn from the Fantasy Flight Games announcement:

- **The card:** Cad Bane, Still Faster Than You
- **The format:** Premier
- **Effective:** 24 August 2026
- **Metagame share:** over 20 per cent
- **Conversion:** described as converting to top cut extremely well
- **The verdict:** "a development mistake"

The reasoning given is worth reading carefully, because it is more candid than these announcements usually are. FFG state that they failed to identify Cad Bane as the strongest leader in its colour combination during playtesting, and that they underestimated the aggressive strategies capable of beating control-focused matchups.

Note what the announcement does not contain: a specific win rate percentage. The published justification rests on play rate plus top-cut conversion, not on a quoted win rate figure. We are not going to invent one.

## Why over 20 per cent of the metagame is the number that matters

A deck being popular is not by itself grounds for action. A deck being popular *and* converting well is a different problem, and it is the combination the announcement identifies.

The distinction is worth understanding. High play rate with average conversion usually means a deck is accessible or fun, and formats absorb that. High play rate with high conversion means the deck is both the obvious choice and the correct one, which compresses everything else out of the format. When one leader passes a fifth of the field on those terms, the practical effect is that other decks are not being beaten so much as not being played.

That is what the announcement means by keeping "an untold number of decks almost entirely out of the meta." The cost is not just to the decks that lost to Cad Bane. It is to the decks nobody bothered to bring.

## Suspension versus ban: what the difference actually is

This trips people up, so here is the honest version.

A **suspension** is presented as a temporary removal, with the expectation of future review. A **ban** is presented as permanent. In practice the immediate effect on your ability to play the card in Premier is identical: you cannot.

The announcement does not commit to a return date or to specific criteria for reinstatement. It indicates ongoing monitoring, with the Homeworlds meta check-in named as the next review point. Homeworlds is expected in October 2026.

For a card to return, the format would need to change enough that a leader FFG has described as a development mistake becomes reasonable. That is a high bar, and the practical planning assumption for anyone building a deck this season should be that Cad Bane is not coming back soon.

## Winners and losers

We want to be careful here, because two days is not enough time for results, and everything in this section is expectation rather than observation.

**Structurally likely to benefit:** aggressive strategies were, per the announcement, part of what should have kept Cad Bane in check and did not. With the leader gone, the decks that were being squeezed out of a field where a fifth of opponents played one card now have room. That is a broad benefit rather than a specific one.

**Structurally likely to be hurt:** decks whose entire reason for existing was beating Cad Bane. Every dominant deck creates a counter-deck, and counter-decks have no purpose once the target is removed. If you built specifically to beat Cad Bane, your deck is now solving a problem nobody has.

**The genuinely unknown:** which leader inherits the top spot. We have written up what the early evidence supports in [the Star Wars Unlimited meta after Cad Bane](/blog/star-wars-unlimited-meta-after-cad-bane/), and the honest headline there is that two days of data cannot answer it.

## What this means for Australian players

Three practical points.

1. **Check your event's format.** The suspension applies to Premier. If your local store runs other formats, confirm what applies there rather than assuming.
2. **If you own the suspended leader, do not panic sell.** The competitive premium is gone, but showcase and serialised printings hold collector value that a format decision does not erase. Selling into the two days after an announcement is generally the worst available timing.
3. **If you were about to buy in, wait.** Buying a suspended leader for Premier play makes no sense, and the rest of the format has not settled enough to know what to buy instead.

## What happens next

The stated next checkpoint is the Homeworlds meta check-in, with Homeworlds expected in October 2026. A new set arriving is the most likely thing to change the calculation, both because it reshapes the format and because it is the moment FFG have flagged for review.

Between now and then, the things worth watching are which leader actually takes the top share, whether the format genuinely diversifies or simply replaces one dominant deck with another, and whether the aggressive strategies FFG referenced actually materialise.
