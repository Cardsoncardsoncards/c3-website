Eldritch Moon rounded out the Shadows over Innistrad story in July 2016, packing the set with transformation mechanics, meld cards and a heavy dose of Innistrad's gothic horror. It remains a set that Commander players return to for value pieces rather than headline mythics, and this list tracks that pattern using EDHREC rank, where a lower number means a card sees more play in the format.

Every card below appears once on the list. Where a card has more than one printing within Eldritch Moon, the ranking uses its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank, and the price shown is that of its highest priced printing. If two printings of the same card land on the same price, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved. In this particular set, every card here has a single priced printing, so there is no splitting of prices to untangle.

Prices below are given in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Singles markets move constantly, and a card's price the week this was written will not necessarily be its price when you read it. ## Frequently asked questions

**When did Eldritch Moon release?**
Eldritch Moon released on 22 July 2016 as the second set in the Shadows over Innistrad block.

**What does this ranking measure?**
It orders Eldritch Moon cards by EDHREC rank, a measure of how often each card is played in Commander decks, with a lower rank indicating more play.

**How are cards with multiple printings in the set handled?**
Each card appears once on the list using its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank, and it is priced using its highest priced printing. If two printings land on the same exact price, that tie is noted rather than resolved silently, though in this list every card has only one priced printing.

**Which card is the most played Eldritch Moon card in Commander?**
Geier Reach Sanitarium tops the list at EDHREC rank 436, priced at AU$5.02.

**Are the prices listed here fixed?**
No, every price quoted is a snapshot taken at one point in time. Singles prices move constantly, so figures will shift after publication.

**Why do some cheaper cards rank higher than expensive ones?**
EDHREC rank tracks how often a card is used in decks, not its market price, so a low cost common can outrank an expensive mythic if it appears in more decklists.
