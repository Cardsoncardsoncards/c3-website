The single most distinctive thing about Pokemon 30th Celebration is that every booster pack contains one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations. That turns the set into a defined collecting objective rather than a lottery, and it is the feature most likely to keep people opening packs long after release week. Twenty of the thirty are currently confirmed. This is the running checklist, and it includes something useful: the card numbering tells you exactly where the missing ten sit.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** The set releases 16 September 2026 and the full card list is not officially complete. This checklist includes only cards confirmed in the [C3 Pokemon Card Vault](/cards/pokemon). We are not filling the gaps from leaks or speculation. Where a card is unconfirmed, it is listed as unconfirmed.

## Quick answer

Twenty of the thirty Pikachu illustrations are confirmed, running through the 023 to 050 range of the main set numbering. The numbering has exactly eight gaps inside that range, at 024, 027, 029, 031, 035, 045, 046 and 048, which almost certainly hold eight of the ten missing Pikachu. Separately from the thirty, there are four Pikachu ex cards: two Double Rares at 053 and 054, and two Special Illustration Rares at 149 and 150. None of the thirty carries a price yet, because the set has not released.

## How the mechanic works

Confirmed structure, from the official product information:

- Each booster pack contains **five foil cards**
- One of those five is always **one of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations**
- Plus **one foil Basic Energy** card
- Every card in the set is foil

The important consequence for collectors: your progress toward the thirty depends only on how many packs you open, not on which product those packs came from. No Elite Trainer Box, Ultra-Premium Collection or Pokemon Center exclusive improves your rate. This is a pack-linked mechanic, so the cheapest cost per pack is strictly the best route.

That makes the [Booster Bundle at AU$130.31](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-booster-bundle) the sensible Pikachu-chasing purchase and the [AU$770.12 Ultra-Premium Collection](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-30th-celebration-ultrapremium-collection-night) a poor one for this specific goal. We work through the full product comparison in the [30th Celebration product guide](/blog/pokemon-30th-celebration-product-guide-australia/).

## Where the missing ten are

This is the part you will not find on other checklists, and it comes straight from reading the numbering rather than from any leak.

The confirmed twenty occupy a block from 023 to 050. Inside that block there are exactly **eight missing numbers**:

**024, 027, 029, 031, 035, 045, 046, 048**

A contiguous numbered run with eight gaps in it, in a set where thirty Pikachu are confirmed to exist and twenty are visible, is about as strong a structural signal as you get before an official reveal. Those eight slots are almost certainly Pikachu that have not been publicly revealed yet.

That accounts for 28 of the 30. The remaining two most likely sit immediately after 050, at 051 and 052, since 053 and 054 are already occupied by Pikachu ex cards.

**We want to be clear this is inference, not confirmation.** It is a reading of the numbering pattern in C3's data, not an official statement, and the last two in particular are the least certain part of it. But if you are building a binder page or a checklist now, leaving those slots open is a better bet than assuming the twenty confirmed cards are numbered arbitrarily.

The official description is of thirty unique Pikachu illustrations guaranteed one per pack, and these four sit outside the 023 to 050 numbering block where the confirmed twenty live. Our reading is that the thirty are the base illustrations and the Pikachu ex cards are additional, but that is inference.

The two Special Illustration Rares at 149 and 150 are the ones collectors will chase hardest regardless of how they are counted. Special Illustration Rares of popular Pokemon have historically been among the strongest performers in any set, and Pikachu in an anniversary set is about as popular as it gets.

## Why no prices yet

None of the thirty confirmed Pikachu carries a price in the Card Vault, and that is expected rather than a data gap. The set does not release until 16 September 2026. Sealed products have preorder pricing because they can be pre-sold as units. Individual cards from an unreleased set generally do not, because there is nothing to sell.

Expect prices to appear in the days after release. The pattern to watch: with every card in the set foiled and one Pikachu guaranteed per pack, the base thirty will be abundant. Abundance is the point of this mechanic. Do not expect individual base Pikachu to be expensive, and be sceptical of anyone telling you they will be.

The scarcity, such as it is, sits in the Special Illustration Rares and in the two [Futuristic Rares](/cards/pokemon/me-30th-celebration-mewtwo-ex-157-128) elsewhere in the set.

## How to collect all thirty

**Buy packs, not boxes.** The mechanic is pack-linked. Cost per pack is the only variable that matters.

**Expect duplicates, and plan for trading.** Thirty cards drawn one per pack means the back end of the set takes considerably longer than the front. The last few are always the expensive ones in time, not in money.

**Singles will be the efficient finish.** For a bounded set of thirty abundant cards, buying the final handful as singles after release is almost always cheaper than opening toward them. Check the [C3 Pokemon Card Vault](/cards/pokemon) once pricing appears.

**Do not pay pre-release premiums for base Pikachu.** They are guaranteed one per pack in a globally simultaneous release. They will not be scarce.

**Storage.** Thirty cards is one and a bit standard binder pages at nine per page, or exactly three rows of ten if you want the display to read as a complete set. Every card in this set is foil, so sleeve them: foils scratch and curl more readily than non-foils, and a scratched foil in a display set is obvious.

## How this compares to previous Pikachu collecting

Pokemon has run Pikachu-focused subsets before, and the difference here is structural rather than one of scale.

Previous Pikachu chases have generally worked on rarity: a small number of Pikachu cards at high rarity, pulled against long odds, with most of a set's Pikachu presence being ordinary cards. The result is that completing the Pikachu you actually wanted was a matter of luck or of buying singles at chase prices.

30th Celebration inverts that. Thirty Pikachu, one guaranteed in every pack, at what appears to be a uniform base treatment. Completing the set becomes a function of volume and patience rather than of luck or money. That is a meaningfully friendlier collecting objective, and it is why we expect this mechanic to sustain engagement past the usual release-week spike.

The trade-off is on the other side of the ledger. A guaranteed card is an abundant card, and abundant cards do not become valuable. If your interest in the thirty is as an investment rather than as a collection, the mechanic that makes them fun to collect is the same mechanic that caps what they will be worth. Both things are true at once, and it is worth being clear-eyed about which one you are here for.

## Australian notes

The 16 September release is simultaneous worldwide, the first time Pokemon has done this, so Australian collectors are not waiting behind other markets for the first time in the game's history.

The practical Australian advice is unglamorous: buy the cheapest packs you can find, be patient about the last few, and finish with singles.
