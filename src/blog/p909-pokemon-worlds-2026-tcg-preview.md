The 2026 Pokemon Trading Card Game World Championships begin in two days. The Masters Division field arrives in San Francisco playing the first Worlds of the post-rotation Mega Evolution format, and The Pokemon Company has already published its own power rankings for the event. This preview covers what those rankings say, which cards actually carry each deck, and what the whole watchlist costs in Australian dollars, because almost all of it is cheaper than people expect.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** No games have been played. Deck rankings below are The Pokemon Company's own published pre-event power rankings, not results, and they are labelled as such throughout. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices, which convert TCGplayer market prices at the daily rate and track the US market rather than Australian shopfronts.

## Quick answer

Worlds 2026 runs 28 to 30 August at the Moscone Center in San Francisco, with the Grand Finals at the Chase Center on 30 August. The official power rankings put Dragapult ex first, followed by Festival Lead, N's Zoroark ex, Mega Excadrill ex and Slowking. Dragapult has led the format since the April rotation and has been reported at roughly a quarter of the Japanese field. The format is Standard under the post-rotation card pool, so the 30th Celebration set does not apply: it releases 16 September, more than a fortnight after the trophy is handed out.

## The format you need to understand first

The 2026 Standard rotation took effect on 10 April 2026 for in-person events, and 26 March 2026 on Pokemon TCG Live. Cards carrying the G regulation mark left the format. H, I and J marks remain legal, which means the Mega Evolution block is the foundation of everything at this tournament.

That rotation matters for reading the power rankings. Dragapult ex is described in coverage as the only major archetype that came through the rotation largely intact, which is a substantial part of why it has led the format all season rather than any single new card. If you want the fuller picture of how the rotation reshaped the game here, we covered it in [the Pokemon TCG rotation explained for Australian players](/blog/pokemon-tcg-rotation-australia-explained/).

## The cheapest headline watchlist in years

Add up every card named above and the total is roughly AU$45 across all five decks, and that figure is dominated by one ACE SPEC. The most expensive individual card in the entire official power rankings costs less than a booster pack of most sets.

This is a genuinely unusual state for a Worlds format. The expensive printings of these cards absolutely exist, and some are steep: the Special Illustration Rare [N's Zoroark ex](/cards/pokemon/me-ascended-heroes-ns-zoroark-ex-286217-286-217) is AU$216.49 and the Special Illustration Rare [Lillie's Clefairy ex](/cards/pokemon/me-ascended-heroes-lillies-clefairy-ex-280217-280-217) is AU$233.90. But those are collector prices for the same gameplay. Nothing in the competitive core requires them.

## What could actually happen

The official preview makes this point itself, and it is worth repeating rather than burying: the most memorable Worlds tend to be won by a deck nobody ranked. Gardevoir ex won the 2025 World Championship and Iron Thorns ex won in 2024, and neither was a top-ranked pick beforehand.

The specific ways this event could diverge from the rankings:

- **Dragapult gets targeted.** A quarter of a field playing one deck is also a quarter of a field that everyone else has built to beat. High representation and high conversion are different things.
- **Pitch Black is only six weeks old.** Mega Excadrill ex is the least-tested archetype on the list, which cuts both ways: less refined, but also less solved by opponents.
- **Festival Lead is a lead deck.** Strategies that depend on executing a specific opening are punished hardest by best-of-three sideboarding across a long day.
- **Regional metagames differ.** The reported 25 per cent Dragapult figure is Japanese data. Worlds is not a Japanese tournament.

## Watching from Australia

San Francisco runs on Pacific Daylight Time during August, which is 17 hours behind Australian Eastern Standard Time. In practice that means a mid-morning start in San Francisco lands in the small hours of the following morning on the Australian east coast, and the Sunday Grand Finals at the Chase Center will play out across Monday morning here.

For most Australian viewers that makes the sensible approach catching the finals on replay rather than staying up for Swiss rounds. The more useful thing to watch live, if you are going to watch anything live, is the Day 2 deck breakdown, because that is when the actual metagame becomes visible rather than predicted.

## Why the post-Worlds week matters more than the event

Worlds is the single largest annual price event in the Pokemon TCG secondary market, and the mechanism is straightforward. A deck wins, thousands of players decide to build it, and demand for a handful of specific cards arrives all at once against a supply that cannot respond for weeks.

The cards most exposed to that here are the ones that appear across multiple ranked decks rather than in one. [Unfair Stamp](/cards/pokemon/sv06-twilight-masquerade-unfair-stamp-165-167) is the obvious candidate at AU$18.59, since it is already the priciest card on the watchlist and ACE SPEC slots are singular. [Genesect ex](/cards/pokemon/sv-black-bolt-genesect-ex-067-086) and [Meowth ex](/cards/pokemon/me03-perfect-order-meowth-ex-062088-062-088) are the next tier of exposure.

None of that is a prediction that these cards will rise. It is a statement about which cards have the least slack if they do. If you already know you want them, the week before a result is generally a better buying window than the week after.

## What it means for Australian players

Three practical points.

1. **The 30th Celebration set is not part of this.** It releases 16 September 2026, well after Worlds, so nothing in it can appear here. If that is what you are actually shopping for, start with our [30th Celebration set guide](/blog/pokemon-30th-celebration-complete-set-guide/) instead.
2. **Post-Worlds is a real price event.** Cards from the winning list routinely move within days as players rebuild. The watchlist above is worth having priced before Sunday rather than after.
3. **Your next local event uses the same format.** Everything here is Standard-legal for Australian play now, not a special Worlds-only pool.
