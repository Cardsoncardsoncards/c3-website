Scars of Mirrodin launched on 1 October 2010, dropping players into a Phyrexian invasion of the artifact-heavy plane of Mirrodin and leaving behind a card pool that still turns up in Commander decks today. This list ranks the twenty most played cards from the set according to EDHREC rank, where a lower number means the card sees more play across tracked decks. It is a look at play frequency, not a valuation guide, though we have paired each entry with current pricing for context.

Every card here has exactly one printing within Scars of Mirrodin itself, so there are no internal ties to break and no secondary printings to weigh against a headline price. Where a set does carry multiple printings of the same card, our method takes the best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank to set position, and prices from the highest priced printing, noting explicitly when two printings land on the same price rather than quietly picking one.

Prices are quoted in Australian dollars and reflect a snapshot at the time of writing. The secondary market moves constantly, so a figure that holds true today may look different in a few months, in either direction. Singles for cards on this list can be sourced through our own card pages or through eBay Australia, and checking both is worthwhile for readers chasing a particular printing.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Scars of Mirrodin release?**
Scars of Mirrodin released on 1 October 2010.

**Which card is the most played card from Scars of Mirrodin in Commander?**
Mox Opal is the most played, sitting at EDHREC rank 240, well ahead of every other card in the set by this measure.

**How does the ranking handle cards with multiple printings in the set?**
Each card appears once, using its best, meaning lowest, EDHREC rank across its printings within the set, and priced by whichever of those printings is highest. Every card on this particular list, however, has only a single priced printing in Scars of Mirrodin, so no such comparison was needed here.

**What happens when two printings of a card share the same price?**
The methodology notes the tie explicitly rather than silently choosing one printing over the other, though this situation did not arise for any card on this list.

**Where can Australian readers buy these singles?**
Singles can be bought through the card pages on our own site, and through eBay Australia.

**Are the prices listed fixed or do they change?**
The prices quoted are a snapshot in Australian dollars at the time of writing. Secondary market prices move over time, so figures should be treated as a point-in-time reference rather than a permanent value.

**Does EDHREC rank measure a card's price or value?**
No. EDHREC rank reflects how frequently a card appears in tracked Commander decks, not its market price, which is why some highly played cards on this list carry low prices and some lower-ranked cards carry higher ones.
