Homelands is one of the more overlooked expansions from the early years of Magic: The Gathering, released on the 1st of October 1995. It is a small set, remembered as much for its distinctive artwork and its awkward reputation among players as for anything else, but it still carries a collector market decades on. This post ranks the twenty most expensive cards from the set by current Australian dollar price, from number one down to number twenty.

The methodology is simple but worth stating clearly. Each card is counted once, even if it has multiple printings within the set. Where a card has more than one priced printing, we take the highest of those prices as the figure that determines its rank. Where two printings share exactly the same price, that tie is noted rather than resolved arbitrarily. In this particular list every card happens to have just one priced printing in the set, so the ranking is a plain ordering of single figures.

It is worth remembering that every price quoted here is a snapshot. Card prices move, sometimes gradually and sometimes quickly, in response to factors well beyond any single list. What is accurate today may look different in a few months, so treat the figures below as a point in time rather than a permanent record.

Koskun Falls sits at the top of the list at AU$23.39, just ahead of Didgeridoo. From there the list runs down through a mix of rares and one common, ending at Aysen Crusader below the two dollar mark.
