Since Bandai revealed Rocks.D.Xebec on 3 August 2026 as the sixth and final OP-17 Leader, he has absorbed more competitive discussion than the other five combined. The question everyone is asking is whether he is already the deck to beat. The honest answer, as at 26 August 2026, is that nobody can know yet, and the reason why is more useful than a guess would be.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** This article is written before any physical OP-17 tournament has been completed. Everything below is labelled by evidence type. Where a claim rests on simulator play, theorycrafting or pre-release testing, it is described as such and is not presented as tournament proof.

## Quick answer

No. Rocks.D.Xebec is not established as the deck to beat, because as at 26 August 2026 there is no completed physical OP-17 tournament anywhere to establish it from. OP-17 has been legal for five days, since the English pre-release on 21 August. Early builds are drawn from the available card pool and simulator play rather than results. Rocks is a credible contender with a coherent game plan and the strongest price signal of any Leader in the set, but credible contender and deck to beat are different claims, and only one of them is currently supported. The first real test is the Brisbane Regional on 5 September 2026.

## The OP16 baseline you are measuring against

To judge whether OP-17 has changed anything, you need to know what it landed on top of. The OP16 format going into this release was, per tier list coverage published 7 July 2026, dominated by Monkey D. Luffy, Enel and Marshall D. Teach, which between them accounted for the majority of top finishes.

Behind that leading group, several decks were established and are not going to evaporate because a new set arrived:

- **Rosinante**, which gained significantly from OP16 through the Sengoku Leader and new Navy cards, with Sakazuki as a late-game finisher.
- **Green Dracule Mihawk**, which gets a power boost against Slash attribute Leaders and can reactivate Don with its Leader effect.
- **Nami**, running Thriller Bark support including Perona and Gecko Moria alongside OP16 additions.
- **Lucy**, a consistent presence in the format.

This is the part most release-week analysis skips. A new set does not reset a format by existing. In the closest comparable case we have covered recently, [Riftbound's Vendetta release](/blog/riftbound-vendetta-meta-first-week/), the new set failed to displace the previous one at all in its first week, and the decks that kept winning were the old ones. That is the base rate, and OP-17 has to beat it rather than being assumed to.

## What Rocks.D.Xebec actually does

The strategic outline is reasonably clear from the card pool, and this part does not require tournament data to describe.

Blue Rocks wants to draw aggressively, convert cards that would otherwise be awkward into defensive resources, slow the opponent's development, and then rebuild a board late through the Secret Rare [Rocks.D.Xebec (118)](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-118-op17-118). It is a resource and attrition plan rather than a rush plan.

The core cards to know:

- [Rocks.D.Xebec (OP17-039)](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-039-op17-039), the Leader, AU$4.25 for the base print
- [Rocks.D.Xebec (118)](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-118-op17-118), the Secret Rare rebuild piece, AU$60.31
- [Are You That Afraid of the New Era?!](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-are-you-that-afraid-of-the-new-era-alternate-art-op17-037), the set's most expensive Rare at AU$169.98 in alternate art
- [Miss Buckingham Stussy](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-miss-buckingham-stussy-alternate-art-op17-054) at AU$50.82
- [Benn.Beckman](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-bennbeckman-alternate-art-op17-027) at AU$49.37

## The other OP-17 Leaders should not be written off

Early attention concentrates, and it is frequently wrong about where. Five other Leaders arrived in this set, each a headline character with a full support package:

- **[Monkey.D.Luffy](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-monkeydluffy-079-op17-079)** in Black, the Leader Bandai foregrounded in its own product copy.
- **[Edward.Newgate](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-edwardnewgate-001-op17-001)** in Red, paired with the set's only Treasure Rare, also a Whitebeard card.
- **[Kaido](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-kaido-058-op17-058)** in Purple.
- **[Shanks](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-shanks-020-op17-020)** in Green.
- **[Charlotte Linlin](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-charlotte-linlin-099-op17-099)** in Yellow.

Early commentary has grouped Blue Rocks alongside Green Mihawk, Red Ace, Red/Black Sabo and Yonko Luffy as the decks to watch. That grouping mixes new OP-17 Leaders with established OP16 archetypes, which is exactly the right shape for a format one week into a release, and it is a reminder that the answer to "what beats Rocks" may well be a deck that already existed.

## The counters and the unknowns

Things that could stop Rocks from becoming the deck to beat:

1. **The attrition plan is slow.** Decks built to draw, defend and rebuild are structurally vulnerable to aggressive starts. If the field arrives at Brisbane full of fast Red decks, the plan is tested immediately.
2. **The field has had five days.** Nobody has a refined list. Early physical results tend to overrepresent whoever built fastest rather than whoever built best.
3. **Established decks are still established.** Luffy, Enel and Teach did not stop working on 21 August.
4. **Hype creates its own counterplay.** A Leader this visible gets targeted in sideboards and deck selection before it has proven anything.

The genuine unknowns are the ones nobody can resolve from here: real matchup spreads, refined list composition, and whether the Rocks rebuild plan holds up across a long Swiss day against opponents who have prepared for it.

## How to test Rocks yourself before Brisbane

If you want an actual opinion rather than someone else's, the barrier is unusually low this time, and that is the most actionable thing in this article.

The base-print [Rocks.D.Xebec Leader](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-039-op17-039) is AU$4.25. The single most expensive card you genuinely need is the Secret Rare [Rocks.D.Xebec (118)](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-118-op17-118) at AU$60.31 in its base printing, and even that has a cheaper path: the alternate art at AU$149.34 is the same card, so buy the base version. Everything else in the shell sits in the commons and uncommons, where the entire tier tops out at AU$11.34 and AU$22.52 respectively.

What to pay attention to while testing, in priority order:

1. **Does the draw engine actually convert into board presence, or just into cards?** Attrition decks fail when they accumulate resources they never deploy.
2. **How does the game go when you are on the back foot by turn three?** This is the aggressive-start question, and it is the one most likely to decide whether Rocks is real.
3. **Is the 118 rebuild a win condition or a consolation?** If games are already decided by the time it lands, the deck needs restructuring rather than refinement.

A week of honest games against the OP16 decks that are actually in your local field will tell you more than any tier list published this month, and for under AU$70 of singles it is the cheapest competitive information available right now.

## What Brisbane will settle

The [One Piece Card Game Championship 2026 Brisbane September Regional](/blog/one-piece-op17-complete-card-guide/) runs Saturday 5 September 2026 at the Brisbane Convention and Exhibition Centre, organised by TAK Games, as a constructed Swiss event. It is the first substantial physical OP-17 dataset that Australian players will get.

The specific things to watch:

- **Raw representation.** How many players actually sleeved Rocks, which measures expectation rather than performance.
- **Conversion.** What share of Rocks decks made top cut relative to their share of the field. This is the number that separates a popular deck from a strong one, and it is the one to hold out for.
- **Whether OP16 decks hold.** If Luffy, Enel and Teach still fill the top cut, OP-17 has added options rather than reset the format.
- **List convergence.** Whether the successful Rocks lists look like each other.

We will cover the results once verified standings and enough decklists are available.

## The C3 take

Rocks.D.Xebec is the most interesting Leader in OP-17 and probably the most likely of the six to matter competitively. He is not the deck to beat, because as at 26 August 2026 there is no physical result in existence that could make him one. Anyone telling you otherwise is describing a prediction.

The practical consequence for Australian players is cheerful: the base-print Leader is AU$4.25 and the core is inexpensive, so you can test the deck yourself before Brisbane at close to no cost.
