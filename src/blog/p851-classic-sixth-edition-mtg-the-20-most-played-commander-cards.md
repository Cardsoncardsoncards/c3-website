Classic Sixth Edition landed on the 21st of April, 1999, as the first of the modernised core sets, trimming the old power level down and setting the template for the core sets that followed. It's a set full of cards that have quietly stuck around in Commander decks for two decades, from mana-fixing lands to a handful of tutors that show up in cube and constructed alike. This list ranks the 20 most played cards from the set in Commander according to EDHREC rank, where a lower number means the card sees more play.

Every card here appears once. Where a card had several printings within this set, the methodology takes its best (lowest) EDHREC rank for placement and its highest priced printing for the headline figure, and if two printings land on exactly the same price, that tie is called out rather than resolved by guesswork. In this particular set most entries carry only a single priced printing, so the headline figure and the card's only listed price are one and the same.

Prices are quoted in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Singles markets move, sometimes by the week, so treat every figure here as a point in time rather than a promise. Readers can track down copies through the card pages on our own site or through eBay Australia, and it's worth checking both before buying.

What follows is the ranking itself, from the single most played card in Commander down to the twentieth, with a short note on each entry beyond the numbers already printed above it.

The set released on the 21st of April, 1999.

Each card appears once, ranked by its best (lowest) EDHREC rank and priced at its highest priced printing; if two printings share a price exactly, that tie is noted rather than resolved silently. In this particular list, every card had only a single priced printing within the set.

**Is this list ranked by price or by play frequency?**
By play frequency, specifically EDHREC rank in Commander, where a lower rank number means the card is played more often. Price is reported alongside but does not determine the order.

**Does higher rarity mean a higher rank on this list?**
Not necessarily. Several uncommons and commons, such as Rampant Growth and Llanowar Elves, rank higher in play frequency than some of the rares further down the list.
