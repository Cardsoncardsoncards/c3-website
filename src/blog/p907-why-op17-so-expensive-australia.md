On 26 August 2026, two days before the English wide release of OP-17 The World's Strongest Warriors, the same English booster box was advertised by Australian retailers at AU$219.99 and at AU$749.99. That is not a small variation between shopfronts. It is a 3.4 times spread on an identical product, and the cheaper listings are already gone. This article works through what is genuinely driving that, what is hype without evidence behind it, and how to decide what you should actually pay.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** Australian retail figures below are advertised asking prices captured on that date. An asking price is not a completed sale, and we have not treated it as one. Card prices are live C3 Card Vault figures, which are TCGplayer market prices converted to Australian dollars at the daily rate and therefore track the US market rather than Australian resale.

## Quick answer

Four things are pushing OP-17 prices up, and only three of them are real. The 4th anniversary status and the Yonko-heavy Leader lineup are driving genuine demand. The new global simultaneous release has compressed the buying window from months to days, which concentrates that demand. Australian allocation is visibly tight, with multiple retailers sold out before release day. The fourth factor, the widely quoted AU$700-plus box price, is not evidence of the set's value. It is a small number of listings from retailers who still have stock, and it should not be your reference point.

Read that table carefully, because the ordering matters more than the numbers. The two cheapest Australian listings are both unavailable. The only one you can actually buy from is the dearest. That is the signature of a supply squeeze, not of a set that is genuinely worth AU$750 a box.

The C3 tracked figure of AU$413.15 for the [OP-17 booster box](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-the-worlds-strongest-warriors-booster-box) is a useful anchor precisely because it is not an Australian shopfront price. It reflects broader market pricing converted to Australian dollars, and it sits almost exactly halfway between the two extremes.

## What is genuinely driving demand

### The 4th anniversary set does real work

Bandai has positioned OP-17 explicitly as the 4th anniversary booster. Anniversary sets in this game have historically carried heavier premium treatments and stronger collector attention than a standard numbered set, and OP-17 follows that pattern: six Leaders across all six colours, every one a headline character, plus a Treasure Rare.

That is a confirmed product decision, not speculation. The Straw Hats appear in their Elbaph outfits for the first time in the card game, tied to the Monkey.D.Luffy Leader.

### The Leader lineup is unusually top-heavy

Every one of the six [OP-17 Leaders](/blog/one-piece-op17-complete-card-guide/) is a marquee name. Three are Emperors, and [Rocks.D.Xebec](/cards/onepiece/the-worlds-strongest-warriors-rocksdxebec-039-op17-039) is the man who assembled the crew that produced several of them. Bandai held his reveal back to 3 August 2026, last of the six, which concentrated attention in the final three weeks before release.

Compare that to a typical booster where two Leaders are recognisable and the rest are situational. The breadth here means more players have a reason to want something from the set, and demand that broad is harder to satisfy from a fixed print run.

### The global simultaneous release changed the timing

This is the genuinely new variable, and it is the one most price commentary is getting wrong.

OP-17 is the first main booster to release in Japanese and English within roughly a week, on 22 and 28 August 2026 respectively, instead of the two to three month gap that has applied until now. Historically, English-market buyers had months to watch Japanese prices settle before committing. That gap functioned as a pressure valve.

Removing it does two things. It compresses all the demand into one window rather than spreading it across a season, and it removes the early price signal English buyers used to rely on. Both push short-term prices up. Neither tells you anything about where the set settles in three months. We covered the mechanics of the change in our [OP17 global release preview](/blog/one-piece-op17-global-release-preview/).

### Australian allocation looks tight

The evidence for this is the sold-out status above rather than any statement from a distributor, so treat it as an inference from observable listings rather than a confirmed supply figure. But two of three Australian retailers checked were out of stock before wide release, and the remaining one is quoting September delivery. That is consistent with constrained allocation.

That distribution matters for a specific reason: **OP-17 is not an expensive set to play. It is an expensive set to complete.** Anyone telling you the set is unaffordable is describing the collector tier, not the competitive one.

If the alternate art Leaders are what you actually want, that is a collector purchase and should be priced as one. ## What is hype without evidence behind it

Two claims are circulating that the current data does not support.

**"Rocks.D.Xebec is the best Leader in the set."** There is no settled physical tournament data on OP-17 at all yet. Early builds are drawn from the available card pool and simulator play. Rocks may well prove dominant, but as at 26 August 2026 that is a prediction, not a result. Our [OP17 early meta piece](/blog/op17-early-meta-rocks-d-xebec/) sets out exactly what the evidence does and does not cover.

**"Boxes will keep going up."** Nobody knows this. The single most reliable pattern in this hobby is that release-week sealed prices for a heavily allocated set are the local peak far more often than they are the floor, because the squeeze is a distribution timing artefact rather than a demand fundamental. It can go either way, and anyone stating it confidently in either direction is guessing.

## A fair-price framework for Australian buyers

Rather than a number, use a test.

1. **Anchor on the tracked market price, not the shopfront.** AU$413.15 is the current C3 figure for the box. If an Australian listing is far above that, you are paying an availability premium, which is a legitimate choice but should be a conscious one.
2. **Price the singles you actually want first.** Add up the base-print Leaders and staples for the deck you intend to play. For most players that total is well under AU$50, which reframes the box decision entirely. [Current OP-17 Leader listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=one+piece+op-17+leader+card&campid=5339146789&customid=op17-expensive-leaders&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) are worth checking against the base-print figures above before you decide a box is necessary.
3. **Treat sold-out listings as information, not as prices.** A AU$219.99 listing you cannot buy tells you what the set was meant to cost, not what it costs.
4. **Do not use the top chase to justify the box.** A AU$3,625 card in the set does not raise the expected value of your box in any way you should plan around. Pull rates on top-end alternate arts are not something to build a purchase on.
5. **Wait if you can.** Release-week supply squeezes on a set with a compressed global window are, structurally, temporary.

You can [compare current OP-17 sealed and single listings on eBay Australia](https://www.ebay.com.au/sch/i.html?_nkw=one+piece+op-17+booster+box&campid=5339146789&customid=op17-expensive-au&mkevt=1&mkcid=1&mkrid=705-53470-19255-0&toolid=10001) against the tracked figures above, and check any individual card against live Australian dollar pricing on the [C3 One Piece Card Vault](/cards/onepiece) before you commit.

## The risks worth naming

- **Thin listing counts.** Several OP-17 Super Rares and Secret Rares have no recorded price at all yet. A headline figure derived from very few listings can move sharply on a handful of sales.
- **The reprint question is open.** Anniversary sets attract demand partly on the assumption of scarcity. Bandai has not made any statement about OP-17 reprints either way, and buying on an assumption nobody has confirmed is a real exposure.
- **Currency.** C3 figures convert from a US-denominated market price at the daily rate. A move in the Australian dollar changes those numbers without anything happening to the card.
- **The set is four days old.** Every figure here is a snapshot taken in the noisiest possible window.

## The C3 take

The expensive part of OP-17 is real but narrow. Six playable Leaders at under AU$5 each sit inside a set whose reputation is being set by a handful of alternate arts and one AU$3,625 outlier. The Australian box price spread is being driven by allocation timing rather than by anything intrinsic to the product, and the shape of the evidence, cheapest listings sold out and dearest listing available, is the classic signature of a squeeze rather than a revaluation.

If you want to play OP-17, it is one of the cheapest sets in years to get into. If you want to complete it, wait for the allocation to normalise.
