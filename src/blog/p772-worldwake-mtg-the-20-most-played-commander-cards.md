Worldwake landed on 5 February 2010 as the back half of the Zendikar block, and more than fourteen years on it still supplies a steady handful of Commander staples. This list ranks the twenty most played Worldwake cards in EDHREC's Commander data, ordered from number 1, the most played card, down to number 20, using EDHREC rank as the measure: a lower rank means a card shows up in more decklists.

Where a card has more than one printing within Worldwake itself, the ranking uses that card's best (lowest) EDHREC rank across those printings, and the price shown is the highest priced version. Any two printings that happen to share a price exactly are noted as tied rather than have one arbitrarily placed above the other. In this particular set every card on the list has just a single priced printing, so the pricing side of the methodology stays simple even though the rule is worth stating.

Prices are quoted in Australian dollars and are a snapshot only. Singles markets move, sometimes by a few cents and sometimes by a lot, so treat every figure here as accurate at the time it was recorded rather than fixed. The most played card in the set, Bojuka Bog, sits at EDHREC rank 24, well clear of the rest of the field.

Australian readers can find these cards through the card pages on our own site, and through eBay Australia, both of which are worth checking against the prices quoted below.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Worldwake release?**
Worldwake released on 5 February 2010 as part of the Zendikar block.

**Which Worldwake card is the most played in Commander?**
Bojuka Bog is the most played card from the set, sitting at EDHREC rank 24, well ahead of every other card on this list.

**How does the ranking handle cards with multiple printings in the set?**
Each card appears once, ranked by its best (lowest) EDHREC rank across its printings, and priced using its highest priced printing. Where two printings share the exact same price, that tie is noted rather than broken arbitrarily.

**Are these prices fixed?**
No, every price quoted here is a snapshot in Australian dollars, and singles markets shift over time, so figures may differ by the time you check them.

**Does a low price mean a card is not played much?**

**Why do some cards on the list have no other printing prices listed?**
