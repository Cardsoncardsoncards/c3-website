The Disney Lorcana North American Championship starts in two days at the Disneyland Hotel, and it arrives with the format in an unusually open state. No deck owns it, the top archetype sits at 18 per cent, and the spread runs from aggressive lore rushers through evasive questers to slower control shells. That makes for a better tournament to watch than a solved format does, and a harder one to predict.

**Data checked: 26 August 2026.** No games have been played. Metagame figures below describe the Core Constructed format going into the event, not results. Australian dollar figures are live C3 Card Vault prices.

## Quick answer

The North American Championship runs 28 to 30 August 2026 at the Disneyland Hotel in Anaheim, California, with 136 players in an invite-only main event, the last 16 places claimed through a Last Chance Qualifier. The format is Core Constructed with Attack of the Vine legal. Amber Amethyst Midrange leads the metagame at 18 per cent, followed by Amber Emerald Elinor and Amber Ruby Boost, with Amber Emerald Songs and Amber Steel Steelsong both at 9 per cent. The striking feature is Amber's presence in every one of the top archetypes.

A 136 player invite-only field is small and strong. That matters for how you read the results: with a field this concentrated, deck choice matters less relative to play skill than it would in a 1,000 player open, and a single archetype's win says less about the format than it would at a larger event.

Two observations worth making.

**No deck dominates.** An 18 per cent leader is a healthy number. Compare the [Star Wars Unlimited situation](/blog/star-wars-unlimited-cad-bane-suspension-explained/), where a leader passing 20 per cent triggered a suspension, or [MTG Standard before the August bans](/blog/mtg-august-2026-ban-list-winners-losers/), where three decks held over half the winner's metagame. Lorcana in mid 2026 is genuinely one of its healthiest competitive states.

**Amber is in everything.** Every named top archetype includes Amber. That is the single most notable structural fact about this format, and it is the thing to watch on the broadcast. A format where one ink appears in every top deck is either extremely well balanced in its other four inks or has a quiet Amber problem, and one weekend of results will tell you which.

The strategic spread is genuinely wide: aggressive lore rushers, evasive questers that go under the opponent, and slower control shells built to answer both. Three distinct plans, none dominant.

**Important caveat about that table.** These are the set's most expensive printings, not necessarily the cards that will decide the tournament. Enchanted and Iconic rarities are alternate treatments of cards that also exist in ordinary printings, and competitive players overwhelmingly use the cheap versions. A card being expensive tells you collectors want it, not that it wins games.

The duo-card theme running through the entire top tier is the clearest design signal in Attack of the Vine, and it is the reason this set has such strong collector pull independent of competitive relevance.

## The three strategies, and what beats what

Because the top of this format splits into three genuinely different plans rather than variations on one, it is worth understanding how they interact. This is general card game structure rather than Lorcana-specific reporting, and it is offered as a framework for watching rather than as sourced fact.

**Aggressive lore rushers** aim to accumulate lore quickly and close the game before the opponent stabilises. They are strongest against slow decks that need time to assemble an engine, and weakest against decks that can trade efficiently in the early turns without falling behind.

**Evasive questers** go under the opponent rather than through them, using characters that are difficult to interact with to accumulate lore steadily. They tend to beat decks built to win board fights, because they decline the fight, and they lose to decks that ignore the board entirely and race.

**Control shells** answer both by trading resources efficiently and winning long games. They beat aggression when they survive the early turns, and they struggle against evasive strategies that do not present targets worth answering.

That produces a rough triangle rather than a hierarchy, which is precisely why no deck is running away with the format. The interesting question for the weekend is which corner the field over-prepares for.

**Why a 136 player invite-only field sharpens this.** In a large open event, the field contains enough inexperienced deck choices to blur these relationships. In an invite-only field, everyone has arrived by winning, everyone has tested, and deck selection becomes a genuine metagame call rather than a preference. If the field collectively expects aggression, the control shells are correctly positioned, and vice versa. That second-order guessing is what actually decides tournaments at this level.

## What could actually happen

- **Amber gets checked.** If a non-Amber deck wins a field where every top archetype played Amber, that is the story of the weekend.
- **A 136 player field is small.** Variance is high. One deck going 8-1 in a field this size is much weaker evidence than the same record in a large open.
- **Attack of the Vine is six weeks old.** The format has had time to settle but not to be solved, which is when surprises happen.
- **The control shells are the wildcard.** Slower decks generally improve as a field becomes better-defined, and an invite-only field is about as well-defined as Lorcana gets.

## Why this event matters more than most

Two reasons beyond the trophy.

**It is the first of a pair.** The European Championship follows on 11 to 13 September 2026, three weeks later, under what should be the same card pool. Two large events that close together, in different regions, on the same format, is the closest thing competitive Lorcana gets to a controlled experiment. If Amber dominates both, that is a much stronger statement than either result alone.

**It is the format's first real stress test since Attack of the Vine.** The set has been legal for six weeks, which is long enough for lists to mature but not long enough for the format to be solved. An invite-only field arriving at exactly that point is when genuinely new deck ideas tend to surface.
