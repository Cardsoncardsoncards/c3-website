Magic: The Gathering | Marvel Super Heroes released globally on 26 June 2026, and it has now been on shelves for five weeks. That is enough time for the initial opening rush to settle and for a clearer secondary market picture to start forming, without yet being so long that early trends have fully locked in.

## What the set actually is

Marvel Super Heroes is a Universes Beyond set, meaning it brings Marvel's characters into Magic without being legal in Standard tournament play. It ships with more than 600 unique cards and four dedicated Commander decks, alongside a Beginner Box built around two tutorial half-decks, one themed on Iron Man and one on Captain America, aimed squarely at bringing new and lapsed players back into the game through a franchise they already know.

## Why five weeks is the right check-in point

The first week or two after any major set releases is dominated by opening volume, packs get cracked, prices swing hard in both directions, and none of it reflects where a card will actually settle. Five weeks in, the initial glut has usually cleared and genuine player demand starts to separate from pure speculation. This is the point where it becomes worth asking which cards are holding their price on real demand rather than launch hype.

## What to actually check before buying in further

Rather than guess at specific numbers here, the more useful exercise is checking C3's own Australian dollar pricing directly against what a card was going for in its first week. A card that has held or grown its price five weeks out is behaving very differently to one that spiked on opening day and has been sliding since. Because Marvel Super Heroes is Universes Beyond rather than Standard legal, its price floor is set almost entirely by Commander demand and collector interest rather than competitive necessity, which tends to make the good cards more durable and the mediocre ones fall further than a tournament staple would.

## The Commander angle matters more than usual here

With four dedicated Commander decks shipping alongside the set and no Standard legality to prop up otherwise mediocre cards, this is a set where Commander playability is doing almost all of the work on long-term price. If a card has not found a home in Commander decks by five weeks in, that is a meaningful signal on its own, regardless of how flashy the artwork or the character is.

## The Beginner Box angle is worth watching separately

The Iron Man and Captain America tutorial half-decks in the Beginner Box are aimed squarely at players who are new to Magic entirely, or lapsed players coming back through a franchise they already know. That is a different audience to the Commander decks, and it is worth tracking as its own signal. If those half-decks are still selling steadily five weeks on rather than sitting on shelves, that tells you the set succeeded at its actual stated goal of bringing new players in, which matters for how Wizards approaches future Marvel-themed Magic products regardless of how any individual chase card performed.

## The honest takeaway at five weeks

There is no single number that tells the whole story for a set like this, and anyone promising a definitive verdict this early is getting ahead of the data. What is genuinely useful at the five week mark is comparing today's price against week one for anything you already hold, and using that trend, not the headline hype, to decide whether to buy more, hold, or move on. That is a slower, less exciting approach than chasing whatever card is trending on social media this week, but it is the one grounded in what has actually happened rather than what people expect to happen.
