March of the Machine landed on the 21st of April 2023, closing out the Phyrexian invasion storyline with a set built around battle cards and a run of double-faced legendary threats. It gave Standard and Commander players a dense block of mythic rares in a short span, and the aftermarket for those cards has settled into a fairly wide spread between the cheapest chase mythic and the bulk-bin commons. This list ranks the twenty most expensive cards from the set by their price in Australian dollars, from the priciest down to twentieth.

Several of these cards were printed more than once within March of the Machine itself, whether as a normal version and a borderless or extended-art alternative, or across the set's battle-card configurations. Where a card has multiple printings, this list uses only the highest priced version to set its rank, and the other printing's price is shown alongside it for comparison. Where two printings land on the same price to the cent, that tie is called out rather than quietly resolved one way or another.

Every price below is a snapshot taken at one point in time. Magic singles move constantly with reprints, supply, tournament results and simple market drift, so the figures here will not match what you see on a card page next week or next month. Treat the list as a read on relative standing within the set rather than a fixed reference.

For buying and selling, Australian collectors mostly work through our own card pages here on Cards on Cards on Cards, or through eBay Australia for wider seller reach. Both markets are reflected in the kind of pricing swings you will see if you check back on these cards over time.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did March of the Machine release?**
March of the Machine released on the 21st of April 2023, and it has been available since that date.

**Which card is the most expensive in the set?**

**How does this list handle cards with multiple printings?**
Each card appears once, ranked by whichever of its printings carries the highest price. Where two printings share the exact same price, that tie is noted rather than resolved silently.

**Why do some cards show three printings but only two prices?**
A few battle cards were printed in three versions within the set, but only two of those versions currently carry a tracked price, so the ranking is based on the two that do.
